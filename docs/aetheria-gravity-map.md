- **B9. What drag means for orbits.** Holding a circular orbit at speed v costs a tangential thrust of Drag·v against drag, plus whatever radial thrust the field mismatch needs. At v = 6.3 (today's rail speed) that is 0.63 u/s² for a Longinus (2-6% of its range), 1.26 for a Djinni (14-37%); at v = 17.5 it is 1.75 (5-16%) and 3.5 (39-100%). No free orbit exists for any hull with Drag > 0; with honest wells a ship in orbit needs only the drag term, where today it also fights a radial mismatch of 17-30% of thrust (probe F2).

## Rationale

- Why the drawn grid is a lowering, not an owner: `grid-shows-how-you-move` makes the slope a promise about motion. Six brush kinds on the gravity layers have no sim term or a different envelope (B3), so today the grid shows motion that does not happen (sector noise, wormholes, rim fades) and hides motion that does (the bowl in small zones is drawn at the same depth everywhere while felt only under the gate).
- Why rails follow the field: every shipped game where ships feel gravity derives the rails from the same parameter (KSP) or simulates bodies (Outer Wilds); authored rails coexist only with no felt gravity (Elite, Starsector). See the prior-art doc, section 3.
- Why strength, softening and tide are separate questions: a·r = v² ties felt strength to body speed (field-strength); c(mass) sets both readability and how many moons a ship can follow (well-strength-from-mass, B10); R sets the peak (softening-radius). Any two can be ruled without the third.
- Why the force gate is not in the subtraction cut: deleting the gate changes forces everywhere the gate zeroed them (every core, every rim), so it is behaviour, not subtraction, and the function it lives in (`GravityAcceleration`) is deleted whole by the potential cut. Removing it first would rewrite FloatingBodyTests' gate assertions into a no-gate test of a law that dies one cut later.
- Why the bowl fixtures become a well and not a new test helper per file: B14 makes the substitution exact, so the subtraction cut changes no expected number in five behaviour tests; the potential cut then replaces the fixture's well law once, in one helper.
- Why orbits are placed at build in a sibling: it touches the zone constructor, Update and the dead run-time orbit writers (B17), none of which the subtraction cut touches, and it removes the stacked-wells-at-origin state before the potential cut needs a valid field at construction.
- Why the potential cut is not split into the potential and its readers: the readers are three lines (Ship's force and tilt, Zone.Flow's downhill); floating bodies, clouds and mines already call GetForce. Deleting GravityAcceleration forces Ship's change in the same commit, so a split would leave a cut that cannot build.
- Why the reference is per body at the zone radius, not a zone-wide constant: a constant per body never changes as bodies move, so the height of a fixed point changes only because wells moved, never because the reference did; and the edge of the zone sits near zero whatever the body count (B22).
- (Superseded by B31: the core is now the Roche limit.) Why the core was the body's radius and the Roche limit 2.44 body radii: both are mass curves with the body radius's exponent, so core < Roche holds at every mass by construction rather than by a check over the generator's range; 2.44 is the classical fluid Roche coefficient at equal densities, and it opens a moon window for 93-100% of bodies (B25). A smaller multiplier opens more windows but lets moons skim the surface.
- Why the calibration is on the sun's own field: field-strength-ruled names bodies at 12-18 u/s, which is v = sqrt(c) of the parent alone; the summed field ships feel is 2-6 times stronger (B21), which is the fork the question puts to the operator.
- Why grid-lowering takes the Sector Waves noise off the grid now rather than leaving it until grid-noise-presentation: grid-gravity-only-ruled already decides that displacement carries gravity only, and the operator's flight on this branch (funnel-kept-until-flown) is to judge how the field moves her, which slopes that move nothing would blur. Keeping the noise drawn while the samplers read gravity only would need a second height texture (a camera or a compute target) that the next cut deletes. The noise stays in the scene on layer 10, drawn only into the nebula patch height, for grid-noise-presentation to place.
- Why one field quad and not the per-body Gravity Well quads with the new law: B30. The per-body quads also cost a GameObject, a material instance and a scale per body, and a quad per body has to cover every camera's view because log tails have no support radius.
- Why a period is derived from `GravityField.Acceleration` rather than written as 2πd/sqrt(c): the formula is the tail law spelled a second time and is wrong inside the core (it would run a child faster than any free orbit there); T = 2π·sqrt(d/a) reads the one law, so the core, and any later change to the law, arrive in the rails with no edit (B35's 234 in-core children get a finite solid-body period).
- Why the asteroid periods live on the belt: Pose and BeltTargets must divide by the same float (BeltTargets.cs:62); one array computed at build is one owner, where two calls to the same function are two spellings that only agree by care.
- Why one zone node owns the zone's far field (well-law r3): the conserving property is one global term of the total mass, and a tree with several roots has several. Hierarchical N-body methods put the same fact in structure: Barnes-Hut (1986) and the fast multipole method (Greengard and Rokhlin 1987) keep one root cell holding everything, whose far field is the monopole of the total mass at its centre of mass. So the node sits at the roots' barycentre; it is not an orbit, has no body and no ring group, and gives no rail (roots are stationary, period +inf, so parent-only rails are untouched). A zone with one massive root needs no node: that root keeps its infinite reach and nothing about single-root zones changes.
- Why nesting the light roots under the heaviest is rejected (B52): roots are stationary, so a light sun fixed on the heavy sun's slope sits in a field nothing balances; a ship parked by it drifts toward the heavy sun while the sun's planets keep their rails, which is the coasting-orbit mismatch `tame-tails-parent-only-rails` ruled out. Patched conics (spheres of influence, as KSP flies them) are the prior art for the alternative: each region owned by one body. Isolated subzones are that reading for stationary roots; superposed-inside keeps today's superposition of fixed masses. Which one the operator flies is question `multi-root-zone-shape`.
- Why a belt's extent is the well tree's input and not the placement's alone: "inside L_F a node pulls as its body alone" is the tree's guarantee about every rail round the node, and a belt is a family of rails out to its outermost asteroid (B53). The tree reads that extent; children-within-reach decides where belts go and how wide, and must keep their inner edge outside the Roche margin.
- Why the series threshold is a GravityField constant emitted into the template: B54 sets it at 0.4 by measurement, and a literal `1` in both C# and HLSL is the second spelling `gravity-law-one-spelling` already names.
- Why Settle recurses instead of draining a queue (B59): a node's Settle reads only itself, its parent's chord cap and its own subtree, so the visit order is free, and the order change alone leaves all 1920 zones of seeds 1-30 unchanged. Recursion puts each child's Settle right after its parent places it, so every node settles once by construction. The settled set and the queue's guard then have nothing left to guard; Soul's surviving mutant on that guard (follow-up `queue-settle-guard-pin`) disappears along with it. Why a bodyless member belongs to its node's ring: under `binaries-orbited-from-afar`, nothing orbits a binary except its members (round the node) and children far enough away to see one body. A member that is itself a binary is still a member, and from afar it pulls as one body of its family's mass, the reading `AtLeast` already used. One helper now carries that reading everywhere a mass is read.
- Why the ring's members settle inside their node's Settle rather than the check reading a predicted mass (B58): a member's final mass is whatever its own placement folds, which only its Settle computes; predicting it would be that placement spelled a second time. Settling the members first reads the one owner, and a member's Settle depends only on the Distance and chord cap its node has just written, so nothing else in the breadth-first order moves.
- A constraint for pseudospace, not solved here: a push with curl (B11) has no height, so under `grid-shows-how-you-move` it either never moves ships, or is drawn by another channel, or is restricted to its gradient part.
- Why a belt's shape is its draw plus two rules, not a Shape enum its readers branch on: every reader (Pose, BeltTargets, DrawAhead, mining, the renderer, the minimap) already serves any (Distance, Phase) set (B60). A shape enum read beyond Zone would make each of those readers a second owner of what a shape means.
- Why Trojans ride their rail rather than their own distance: at d ln T / d ln r of about 0.92, any radial width shears the swarm apart within the first hour (B60). Real Trojans are held by libration about L4 and L5 (prior art, section 2.1), which rails cannot produce. The libration-averaged motion is the host's period, so riding the rail is the rail model of a tadpole orbit. The period still comes from the parent's field, read at the guiding centre's distance (`field-owns-orbits`). Libration itself could be added later as a phase wobble.
- Why a Trojan swarm is not the host's child: L4 and L5 lie a chord D from the host, outside its reach of 0.5 D (B60). Rocks orbiting the host would have the wrong motion and sit in the wrong well.
- Why a swarm is not a GeneratorPlanet: it is massless and sits on its host's own rail, so in the placement tree it would be a sibling at the host's distance, claim a slot, and fail station candidacy's RingWindow check against its own host. Emitted beside the host's records after placement, it takes no slot and no candidacy sees it, so the ruling's "must not refuse a host for its own swarm" holds by where the swarm is made, with a test that stations land on swarm hosts (B61). Its random stream is its own (the zone seed with a salt), so turning swarms on or off moves no other draw in the zone.
- Why a lane's width is not a Hill radius: this law's reach does not depend on mass (it is 0.5 D), and Hill's m^(1/3) is not a curve of this law (prior art, section 1.3). Nor is it the placement Roche margin: planets sit above the grid and belts below, so the lane prevents no collision and only sells the impression of a swept lane (ruling `lanes-two-sided-visual-margin`). Its value is laned-belts' to propose and a flight's to judge.
- Why edge material stays out of the extent: otherwise support would pass Gamma x reach and the family's fade band would narrow toward nothing (B60, WellTree.cs:224-229), so Oort-like rocks would change gravity. Their periods read the family's fading pull, which is what the field gives there.
