- **B9. What drag means for orbits.** Holding a circular orbit at speed v costs a tangential thrust of Drag·v against drag, plus whatever radial thrust the field mismatch needs. At v = 6.3 (today's rail speed) that is 0.63 u/s² for a Longinus (2-6% of its range), 1.26 for a Djinni (14-37%); at v = 17.5 it is 1.75 (5-16%) and 3.5 (39-100%). No free orbit exists for any hull with Drag > 0; with honest wells a ship in orbit needs only the drag term, where today it also fights a radial mismatch of 17-30% of thrust (probe F2).
- **B10. Tide under superposed 1/r tails** (computed, `hill.py`, over the probe's bodies). 2D tidal radius of a parent at R from its own parent: d_H = R·sqrt(c_p/(2c_g)). ## Rationale

- Why the drawn grid is a lowering, not an owner: `grid-shows-how-you-move` makes the slope a promise about motion. Six brush kinds on the gravity layers have no sim term or a different envelope (B3), so today the grid shows motion that does not happen (sector noise, wormholes, rim fades) and hides motion that does (the bowl in small zones is drawn at the same depth everywhere while felt only under the gate).
- Why rails follow the field: every shipped game where ships feel gravity derives the rails from the same parameter (KSP) or simulates bodies (Outer Wilds); authored rails coexist only with no felt gravity (Elite, Starsector). See the prior-art doc, section 3.
- Why strength, softening and tide are separate questions: a·r = v² ties felt strength to body speed (field-strength); c(mass) sets both readability and how many moons a ship can follow (well-strength-from-mass, B10); R sets the peak (softening-radius). Any two can be ruled without the third.
- Why the force gate is not in the subtraction cut: deleting the gate changes forces everywhere the gate zeroed them (every core, every rim), so it is behaviour, not subtraction, and the function it lives in (`GravityAcceleration`) is deleted whole by the potential cut. Removing it first would rewrite FloatingBodyTests' gate assertions into a no-gate test of a law that dies one cut later.
- Why the bowl fixtures become a well and not a new test helper per file: B14 makes the substitution exact, so the subtraction cut changes no expected number in five behaviour tests; the potential cut then replaces the fixture's well law once, in one helper.
- Why orbits are placed at build in a sibling: it touches the zone constructor, Update and the dead run-time orbit writers (B17), none of which the subtraction cut touches, and it removes the stacked-wells-at-origin state before the potential cut needs a valid field at construction.
- Why the potential cut is not split into the potential and its readers: the readers are three lines (Ship's force and tilt, Zone.Flow's downhill); floating bodies, clouds and mines already call GetForce. Deleting GravityAcceleration forces Ship's change in the same commit, so a split would leave a cut that cannot build.
- Why the reference is per body at the zone radius, not a zone-wide constant: a constant per body never changes as bodies move, so the height of a fixed point changes only because wells moved, never because the reference did; and the edge of the zone sits near zero whatever the body count (B22).
- Why the core is the body's radius and the Roche limit 2.44 body radii: both are mass curves with the body radius's exponent, so core < Roche holds at every mass by construction rather than by a check over the generator's range; 2.44 is the classical fluid Roche coefficient at equal densities, and it opens a moon window for 93-100% of bodies (B25). A smaller multiplier opens more windows but lets moons skim the surface.
- Why the calibration is on the sun's own field: field-strength-ruled names bodies at 12-18 u/s, which is v = sqrt(c) of the parent alone; the summed field ships feel is 2-6 times stronger (B21), which is the fork the question puts to the operator.
- A constraint for pseudospace, not solved here: a push with curl (B11) has no height, so under `grid-shows-how-you-move` it either never moves ships, or is drawn by another channel, or is restricted to its gradient part.
