## 1. Filament constructions

### 1.1 Cellular / Voronoi edges
1.1.1 [V] F2-F1 is exactly 0 on cell borders but is not a distance: it grows and shrinks with the spacing of the two feature points either side of an edge, so a threshold gives uneven line width (some borders too thin, others swallow the cell). Quilez: "sort of works, but not completely". https://iquilezles.org/articles/voronoilines/
1.1.2 [V] Quilez exact distance-to-border is two passes over neighbour cells: pass 1 searches 3x3 around the shading point for the closest feature point; pass 2 searches 5x5 centred on that point's cell, taking the minimum of the signed distance to the perpendicular bisector, `dot(0.5*(mr+r), normalize(r-mr))`. Cost 9+25 = 34 cell evaluations, constant per pixel, PW. Stated limits: wrong distances near corners where three borders meet; isolines inside cells inaccurate. Same page.
1.1.3 [S] Blender's Voronoi node code credits Quilez's distance-to-edge routine (MIT) and uses a reduced scan window (-1..1 rather than -2..2) as a performance tradeoff. https://developer.blender.org/rB3848507511a7546ab396c80f069b48de3332860f
1.1.4 [S] Worley's basis function is continuous except on Voronoi edges and at seeds (Wikipedia Worley noise).
1.1.5 [S] Heuristic cosmic-web graphics recipe found in search (no primary source): domain-warp coordinates with fBm, then Worley F1/F2 3x3, `smoothstep(width,0,F2-F1)` for filaments, hashed cell sites as halos. Search note: such Voronoi-based models "do not follow a hierarchical multiscale distribution and contain no curved structures".
1.1.6 [V] Jump Flooding Algorithm (Rong and Tan 2006): log2(N) passes for an NxN grid (step N/2 down to 1), each pass 9 samples per pixel; approximate with few small errors; variants JFA+1, JFA+2, 1+JFA lower errors; builds Voronoi, centroidal Voronoi, power diagrams and distance transforms; not covered on the page: weighted seeds. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jump_flooding_algorithm . Original: https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~tants/jfa/i3d06.pdf [S].
1.1.7 [M] Weighted Voronoi: the power diagram (cell by |x-p_i|^2 - w_i) has straight bisector edges and is evaluated pointwise by the same neighbour search only if the search window is widened with the weight range; additively weighted (|x-p_i| - w_i) has hyperbolic edges. Neither derivation checked against a source this pass.
1.1.8 [M] Stability under moving seeds: F1 and F2 are continuous in x and in seed position; F2-F1 and the exact border distance are continuous in position, but the border (zero set) re-routes through topological flips (an edge between two cells disappears, another appears, when four seeds become cocircular), so a thin filament pops at a flip while the field stays continuous.

### 1.2 Distance-field ridges, medial axis
1.2.1 [M] The medial axis of a shape is the ridge/skeleton of its distance transform; for the point set of wells the distance field's ridges are the Voronoi edges (so 1.1 is this case). Ridge detection from a distance field needs second derivatives or a grid; no source read this pass.
1.2.2 [V] Distance-transform costs on GPU: JFA log2(N) passes (1.1.6). Eikonal solvers: fast sweeping needs 2^n directional sweeps in Gauss-Seidel order, ordering limits parallelism (a 2025 paper restructures to row/column parallel); one 2025 paper observes convergence in 2-3 iterations independent of grid size [S single observation]; fast iterative method (Huang 2021) needs no ordering, active list plus remedy list, "runs faster in most cases", iteration scaling not stated in abstract [V abstract]. https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15869 ; search summary https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00035 [S]. Not PW: grid-wide.
1.2.3 [S] Heat method (Crane, Weischedel, Wardetzky, ACM TOG 2013): two linear solves (heat diffusion for a short time, then a Poisson solve on the normalised gradient field); prefactorable; per-vertex approximation of geodesic distance to a source set; converges under refinement. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.6216 . Grid-wide, not PW.
1.2.4 [M] Geodesic (least-cost) paths on a cost surface = ridge/valley of the sum of two eikonal fields (d_A + d_B minimal along the least-cost path between A and B): a way to draw a path between two sources from two distance fields; one pair of eikonal solves per source pair. Derivation not checked.

### 1.3 Source graphs drawn as distance-to-segment fields
1.3.1 [V] Relative neighbourhood graph (Toussaint 1980): edge pq iff no third point is closer to both than they are to each other; EMST is a subgraph; RNG is a subgraph of the Delaunay triangulation; O(n log n) (Supowit 1983), linear from the Delaunay triangulation; the Urquhart graph is a fast approximation. Wikipedia does not discuss locality. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_neighborhood_graph
1.3.2 [V] Gabriel graph: edge pq iff the closed disc with diameter pq is empty; contains EMST, RNG and nearest-neighbour graph; subgraph of Delaunay; linear from Delaunay; not a spanner; per-edge test is a local geometric check, page says nothing on distributed computation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_graph . Both are beta-skeletons [V].
1.3.3 [M] Pointwise evaluation of "distance to nearest graph edge": for n sources the per-edge test is O(n) per candidate pair, so a PW evaluation of the graph is O(n^2)-O(n^3) per point unless sources are bucketed; with sources few per zone (tens) the graph can be computed once per zone change and the field evaluated as min over edges of point-segment distance, O(E) per point. The graph itself is the cache. Not sourced.
1.3.4 [M] Stability: an edge enters or leaves the Gabriel/RNG when a third source crosses the lens or disc, a discrete event; the min-segment-distance field jumps by a segment's width at that instant (pop). EMST edges change when two candidate edge lengths cross. Continuous relaxations (beta-skeleton with continuous beta, soft edge weights) are not sourced.
1.3.5 [S] Tero-style Steiner/minimum-length networks: the Physarum model produced approximations to Steiner minimum trees for up to 16 terminals with low precision; no proven iteration bound for Steiner approximation; improved heuristics claim few iterations experimentally. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.02910 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08233 ; https://www.inderscience.com/offer.php?id=120753 .

### 1.4 Cosmic web: adhesion model, Zel'dovich, geometric construction
1.4.1 [V] Adhesion model = inviscid Burgers dynamics, accepted as a good description of large-scale filamentary structure. Velocity potential psi(x,t) = max_q[psi0(q) - |x-q|^2/(2t)]: at an Eulerian point x, lower an upward paraboloid of curvature radius t on the initial potential psi0(q); the first-contact abscissa is the Lagrangian coordinate q(x,t); simultaneous contact at several points marks a shock at x. Equivalent to the Legendre transform H(x,t) = sup_q[x.q - |q|^2/2 + t psi0(q)] and the convex hull of the Lagrangian potential |q|^2/2 - t psi0(q). https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.1974 (PDF text read); https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0912.3603 (read through char 100k)
1.4.2 [V] In 2D the Eulerian pattern is a Voronoi-like tessellation dual to the Lagrangian convex-hull triangulation: voids = polygons (one per hull vertex, matter-free); walls/filaments = segments separating two polygons (little mass); nodes = vertices where three meet (almost all the mass, proportional to the Lagrangian triangle area). Not a true Voronoi diagram (a planar cut of a higher-dimensional one). Same sources. Density here is a Jacobian of the geometric model; it does not satisfy the continuity equation inside shocks (agrees outside) [V].
1.4.3 [V] Pre-shock regime: the Zel'dovich linear map x = q + t u0(q); the paper warns that setting viscosity to 0 in the Zel'dovich form is not the same as the inviscid limit (multi-flow regions). https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0912.3603
1.4.4 [V] Cost: 1D Burgers solved in O(N) via the convex envelope then Legendre transform (earlier O(N ln N)); the 2D convex hull is O(N_tot ln N_tot) in the cited text, N_tot = N^2 grid points. Needs the global hull at time t: not PW in general. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.1974
1.4.5 [M] The argmax in 1.4.1 for a potential made of discrete weighted sites (psi0 = piecewise from sites with weights w_i) is the power diagram of the sites; evaluating the argmax at a point is O(n) over sites: PW with n sites, and the shock set is where two terms tie, which is the same construction as 1.1 with a time-dependent weight ratio. Derivation, not read in a source. The Lagrangian-to-Eulerian "t" is a free parameter of how far the cells have contracted.
1.4.6 [S] Filament finders for real cosmological data are not constructions but extractors: DisPerSE (discrete Morse theory on a Delaunay density estimate, persistence-filtered; filaments connect onto halos by topology), Bisous (marked point process of cylinders, visit map), T-ReX (tree-based ridge extractor, compared to Bisous/DisPerSE/Nexus), MST/FoF historically. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.4015 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08957 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.00732 . No source found on rendering synthetic filaments between given halos by closed form other than 1.4.1-1.4.2 and 1.1.5.
1.4.7 [S] MCPM/Polyphorm (Elek et al. arXiv 2204.01256): probabilistic generalisation of Jones's agent model, 3D, run on galaxy positions to produce a density map of the web; abstract gives no parameter, iteration or cost figures; page read was abstract only. https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01256

### 1.5 Physarum transport-network model (equations, closed form?)
1.5.1 [V] Model (Tero-Kobayashi-Nakagaki 2007 as restated by Bonifaci, Mehlhorn, Varma): electrical network, edge resistance R_e = L_e / D_e, unit current s0 -> s1, Kirchhoff conservation at nodes (linear system for potentials), diameter dynamics dD_e/dt = |Q_e| - D_e; equilibrium |Q_e| = D_e. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.0423 (PDF text read).
1.5.2 [V] Result: for any graph and initial diameters the network converges to the shortest s0-s1 path (D=1 on the path, 0 elsewhere); convergence of the transportation-problem generalisation is weaker; exponential rate proved only after flow directions stabilise; the paper lists the initial-stage behaviour as open. Same PDF, sections 1, 6, 7 and the open-questions text.
1.5.3 [S] Steady states of the adaptive Hagen-Poiseuille class are non-unique and depend on initial conductivities; Tero 2010 uses dD/dt = f(|Q|) - r D with f a sigmoid/power law (this pass could not open the 2010 Science text: statement from search summary and memory). Continuum form dD/dt = (|grad p| D)^mu - delta D, mu=1 steady state |grad p| = delta or D=0 [S: arXiv 2511.08531]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.12244 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08531 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.10577 .
1.5.4 [V] No closed form: each iteration needs a global linear solve (potentials); an explicit-iteration count to a Steiner-like network is not bounded in any source found. Pointwise: no. Fixed point depends on iteration history.
1.5.5 [M] Few-iteration cheap relatives that are PW: 1.1.2 and 1.3.3 (their outputs are the graphs Jones 2011 says the agent model approximates).

## 2. Layered ecology as a node graph

### 2.1 Ecology models with a DAG-shaped structure
2.1.1 [S] NPZ model: nutrient N, phytoplankton P, zooplankton Z; dN/dt = supply - uptake + recycling; dP/dt = uptake - grazing - mortality; dZ/dt = assimilation of grazing - mortality; grazing Holling II g = gmax P/(K+P) or Holling III (Oschlies and Garcon); spatial form dC_i/dt + div(v C_i) = F_i(N,P,Z) + D_i lap C_i. Zooplankton loss exponent 1 vs 2 changes stability. https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0205009 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.3532 . No single canonical citable form found in this pass.
2.1.2 [M] Steady-state food chain with donor control: each level's density is a monotone function of the level below (e.g. Holling-saturating) times conversion, producing a feed-forward chain N -> P -> Z -> higher predators (DAG); two-way coupling (predator regulates prey) is a cycle, solved only by iteration or equilibrium algebra. From general ecology; no source read.
2.1.3 [S] Nonlocal predator-prey (convolution in prey competition, top-hat or heat kernels) can generate Turing-type stationary patterns not present in the local model and bistability; no Gaussian-kernel analysis found; infinite-support kernel existence of stationary patterns reported open. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.09761 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.03533 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.05224 ; repository.rudn.ru/records/article/record/71933 .
2.1.4 [prior doc] IFD composes pointwise with a global sum (closed-form doc 5.1, 5.5). Trophic IFD for two moving species: no continuous closed form (5.3).

### 2.2 Graph-authored placement in games/tools
2.2.1 [prior doc, V] HZD: per-layer density-map step over mipmapped world maps then dithered discretisation; layers are authored in a graph editor; "data driven, deterministic, locally stable". Search: GDC Vault title and Guerrilla page confirm graph editor, slides listing "Density Based Placement". https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn
2.2.2 [S] Unreal PCG: nodes act on point data and attribute sets; Density Remap D' = (Omax-Omin)(D-Imin)/(Imax-Imin)+Omin; Distance to Density (superseded by the more efficient native Distance node); Curve Remap Density; Attribute Filter; Point Filter and Point Filter Range (can filter by sampling spatial data); Self Pruning removes overlaps by extents/density with a randomised tie-break; Density Noise; Normal to Density; Surface Sampler. https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-content-generation-framework-node-reference-in-unreal-engine
2.2.3 [S] Houdini Scatter SOP density attribute plus Relax Iterations; LOP Scatter Instances Up Axis mask (closed-form doc 3.4).
2.2.4 [S] Gaea: masks are heightmaps, Combine node blend modes (Add, Subtract, Max...), Mask node Blur and Iterations, Accumulator for global masks (Snow, Water, Debris, Trees, Depth, FreshWater), Data Maps (rainfall, sediment); Gaea processes the whole surface then applies the mask. World Machine: "Vegetation Scatter" macro takes a texture plus weightmap; Select Wetness device and erosion flow map used for plant placement; users report blur as the dominant cost in big builds (65k of 85k seconds in one report). https://docs.quadspinner.com/Guide/Graph/Graph.html ; https://forum.world-machine.com/t/very-slow-nodes-any-workaround-faster-alternate-way/3530 ; https://forum.world-machine.com/t/trees-in-world-machine/4726 . No wildlife placement nodes found in either.
2.2.5 [S] Spore: ecological niche = amount of life a planet's ecosystem can support; placement ordered by trophic level (plants before herbivores before carnivores; a carnivore alone with a single herbivore species overhunts it) at generation. Eco (Strange Loop): persistent server simulation of plants and animals with food-chain dependence; marketing-level descriptions only. https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Ecological_niche ; https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/eco-is-an-open-world-survival-game-that-will-let-you-destroy-the-planet . No technical documentation found for either.
2.2.6 [M] Operator vocabulary in these graphs (from 2.2.1-2.2.4): pointwise arithmetic and remap curves, min/max/add/multiply blends, blur or convolution (feeding "nearby" influence), distance to a feature, threshold or range filter, noise modulation, dither or Poisson relaxation to turn density into discrete placements, global-count normalisation. Distance, blur and pruning are the nonlocal operators.

## 3. One graph, two evaluators

3.1 [V] Houdini: VEX cannot run on the GPU; the GPU path is a separately written OpenCL kernel (explicit kernel, bindings, no foreach, thin built-in library, fixed-or-node-dependent precision `fpreal`); data stays on GPU only inside a Compiled Block, every other SOP pulls it back; OpenCL also runs on CPU, so "one algorithm" is the OpenCL one. The page does not say VEX and OpenCL results agree. https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/vex/ocl.html
3.2 [S] Houdini 20.5 added a GPU Volume Blur SOP; forum: heightfield AO OpenCL branch vs VEX branch built side-by-side, the VEX one "very slow"; 144x-192x speedups are single anecdotes.
3.3 [V] Unreal PCG: GPU nodes form Compute Graphs; Custom HLSL runs on GPU only (no CPU execution); GPU output not claimed identical to CPU (weighted mesh selection "similar to the CPU implementation", over-allocation heuristic can drop instances); the doc recommends seeding from position (`ComputeSeedFromPosition()`); virtual-texture sampling needs priming or can be inaccurate; GPU-spawned instances exist only on the GPU and are not saved; CPU/GPU transfers have a cost, Skip Readback to CPU available; feature Beta/Experimental. https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-with-gpu-processing-in-unreal-engine
3.4 [S] Substance Designer: Pixel Processor runs a function per pixel with no knowledge of neighbours; Value Processor computes a single value; the CPU (SSE2) and GPU engines can give different outputs (noise/grain differences) and the vendors advise using the same engine everywhere; CPU engine lacks 16-bit and greyscale 8-bit; no documented bit-exact guarantee. https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/substance-3d-designer/using/substance-graphs/nodes-reference-for-substance-graphs/atomic-nodes/pixel-processor ; https://help.maxon.net/c4d/2026/en-us/Content/html/PREFSSUBSTANCE-PREFS_SUBSTANCE_MAIN_GROUP.html .
3.5 [S] Shader-graph-to-code: Unity Shader Graph maps each node's inputs to variables and calls prebuilt per-node functions (many wrapping HLSL intrinsics); ShaderGen generates HLSL/GLSL/Metal from one C# source. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.17587 ; https://github.com/feliwir/ShaderGen . No source found for generating C# and HLSL from one graph with parity testing.
3.6 [V, local] CultLib research `docs/research/cycles-fma-contraction.md` (git `ae6ef5a8`) records: HLSL `precise` keeps mul and add separate, `mad` may fuse; SPIR-V allows rearrangement and different rounding unless `NoContraction`; GLSL `precise` prohibits arbitrary fma; Vulkan OpFDiv 2.5 ULP, OpFMod inherits `x - y*floor(x/y)`; CUDA `--use_fast_math`; Metal fast math vs safe; `Math.FusedMultiplyAdd` is one rounding, the .NET JIT keeps `a*b+c` as two roundings (dotnet/runtime #10137 closed with explicit-API stance), FMA software fallback misrounding bug (#98704) fixed; DXC/Unity default contraction not confirmed. `F:\Projects\CultLib` (read via git show).
3.7 [V] Hash portability: PCG hash recommended as the default GPU hash (Jarzynski and Olano, JCGT 9(3):21-38, 2020; 32-bit RXS-M-XS; pcg2d etc.); these pages do not state CPU/GPU bit-equality or float-conversion caveats. Integer multiply/add/shift/xor are exactly specified operations [M]. https://www.jcgt.org/published/0009/03/02/paper.pdf ; https://reedbeta.com/blog/hash-functions-for-gpu-rendering
3.8 [S] GPU bilinear texture filtering is fixed-function with undocumented, vendor-varying precision; an RGBA8 texture filtered stays 8-bit; fractional weights are quantised (older hardware reasoning ~6-8 bits); to get exact interpolation load four texels (or Gather) and blend in float32 in the shader; verify empirically. https://gamedev.net/blogs/entry/1696749-tip-of-the-day-bilinear-filtering-accuracy ; https://reedbeta.com/blog/texture-gathers-and-coordinate-precision ; https://community.khronos.org/t/texture-filtering-precision/41917
3.9 [M] Node types by CPU pointwise cost (derived, not sourced):
 - cheap PW in C#, exact tie to shader via same expression: arithmetic, min/max/clamp, remap curves, smoothstep, thresholds, integer hash noise, analytic fields (gravity height, flow), Worley/Voronoi with fixed neighbour window (1.1.2), distance to a small set of points/segments/circles, ideal-free-style ratios if the global sum is a cached constant.
 - need a cache but not a grid: graph of sources (Gabriel/RNG/MST) computed per zone change; global normalisers (sum of resource), per-source constants.
 - force a grid or bounded neighbourhood sampling: blur/convolution of a non-analytic field (cost K^2 samples per point per node; separable halves it), distance transforms, eikonal/geodesic, flow accumulation, ridge detection by derivatives of non-analytic fields, Poisson-disk/dither with neighbour tests, anything that reads another node's value at offsets.
 - analytic closed-form convolution exists only when the input is a sum of Gaussians/radial kernels (blur of a point-source sum is again a point-source sum) [M].
3.10 [prior doc] HZD solved GPU-side density-to-instance via fixed dither threshold per candidate (one sample per thread), evaluated from baked maps; no CPU evaluator described.
