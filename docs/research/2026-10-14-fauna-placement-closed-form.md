## 1. Patchiness from flow: what local quantity predicts aggregation

1.1 Okubo-Weiss. W = s_n^2 + s_s^2 - omega^2 (normal strain, shear strain, relative vorticity), for horizontally non-divergent 2D flow. W>0 deformation (saddle), W<0 rotation (vortex), W=0 needs quadratic terms. Eigenvalues of the velocity-gradient matrix are (gamma +- sqrt(W))/2 with gamma the divergence; complex eigenvalues give spirals that converge if gamma<0. Sign convention varies between sources (py-eddy-tracker docs contradict their own code). PW: needs only the 2x2 velocity gradient at the point. It classifies local flow topology; it does not itself give a density. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okubo%E2%80%93Weiss_parameter (page is flagged there for expert attention). Originals: Okubo 1970 Deep-Sea Res. 17(3):445-454; Weiss 1991 Physica D 48:273-294 [V as citations on that page]. 3D analogues cited there: Q criterion (Hunt, Wray, Moin 1988), Chong-Perry-Cantwell 1990, Jeong-Hussain 1995 (lambda2) [V as citations].
1.2 Okubo-Weiss to plankton link: no source found that ties W to plankton accumulation directly; W is used to split vortex versus strain regions in an island-wake biology paper (arXiv 0802.3532) and a nutrient-flux study (Sci. Rep. 2024 SI) [S]. Known caveats of W as a vortex detector (Eulerian, frame-dependent, misclassifies in time-dependent flows; Basdevant and Philipovitch 1994) [M].
1.3 FTLE: FTLE(x) = log(lambda_max(C))/(2T), C = Cauchy-Green tensor of the flow map over [t, t+T]. Needs advecting particles or a perturbation over T. PI. T is a free parameter. [V] https://lagrangian.readthedocs.io/en/latest/contents.html . Backward-time FTLE ridges approximate attracting LCS; tracer-gradient alignment with backward finite-time Lyapunov vectors is shown for freely decaying 2D turbulence and observed in realistic ocean flows, not presented as general [V, same page].
1.4 FTLE failure modes: ridges capture hyperbolic LCS only under additional conditions; ridges connect high stretching with high shear, so false positives; no reliable detection of elliptic structures. A twelve-method benchmark found false positives and negatives even in mathematically justified methods [V] Hadjighasem et al., "A critical comparison of Lagrangian methods for coherent structure detection", Chaos 27, 053104 (2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05716 (Sec. VI strengths/weaknesses list; also the FTLE paragraph in Sec. II). Haller 2015, Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 47:137-162 (review, not read this pass) [S].
1.5 FTLE/FSLE and plankton, field evidence: d'Ovidio et al. 2004 GRL (Mediterranean mixing structures, FSLE); Lehahn, d'Ovidio, Levy, Heifetz 2007 (stirring of the NE Atlantic spring bloom); a Palmer Deep canyon study reporting phytoplankton patches associated with FTLE ridges, strongest in stratified conditions [S, summaries only]. d'Ovidio et al. 2010 PNAS "Fluid dynamical niches of phytoplankton types" [M]. All need a velocity time series and advection: PI.
1.6 Infinitesimal-time limit (my derivation, [M]): as T goes to 0 the FTLE tends to the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric strain tensor S = (A + A^T)/2 at the point (A = velocity gradient), an instantaneous PW quantity that carries none of the history that builds ridges. The instantaneous growth rate of a passive-scalar gradient is d(grad c)/dt = -A^T grad c, PW given the scalar's gradient.
1.7 Langmuir circulation: counter-rotating wind-aligned cells from the interaction of Stokes drift and vertical vorticity (Craik-Leibovich 1976 equations); surface convergence lines make windrows of buoyant material, divergence zones between them stay clear; spacing from under 2 m to over 1 km, streaks up to 15 km downwind [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langmuir_circulation plus sea-ice and LES papers via search. The operative local quantity is surface convergence -div_h(u); the cell spacing is set by mixed-layer physics, not a local gradient. If the roll field is given analytically, -div_h(u) is PW [M].
1.8 Buoyant-tracer accumulation versus convergence (LES): tracer accumulates in regions of horizontal convergence (downwelling at the surface); less buoyant tracer is submerged more easily and resurfaces in upwelling zones, giving a more uniform distribution; the most buoyant tracer stays near the strongest convergence and reached concentrations up to 50 times those of the least buoyant in the same flow; not every downwelling region fills, convective vortices trap particles in a subset of nodes; clustering is quantified with the Gini coefficient and set by the friction-to-convective velocity ratio and the buoyancy [V] Dingwall, Chor, Taylor, J. Fluid Mech. 954, A27 (2023), doi 10.1017/jfm.2022.969 (text read: abstract, Sec. 1, discussion of figs 3-4). The stated control is particle slip velocity versus peak vertical velocity (about 0.02 m/s there). A simulation result, not a closed form.
1.9 Flotsam on fronts: D'Asaro et al. 2018 PNAS 115, "Ocean convergence and the dispersion of flotsam", cited by that LES paper for accumulation in narrow 10-100 m density fronts [V as citation]; the paper itself not read.

## 2. Divergence as density proxy: when closed forms exist

2.1 Continuity: d(rho)/dt + div(rho u) = 0. Along a fluid trajectory D rho/Dt = -rho div u, so rho(t) = rho0 exp(-integral of div u dt) = rho0 / det(Jacobian of the flow map). This is PI: it needs the trajectory or the time-integrated divergence along it; no pointwise form [M, standard]. Balkovsky-Falkovich-Fouxon treat the same object as the "volume compression factor along the Lagrangian trajectory" in a compressible flow (arXiv chao-dyn/9912027, Phys. Rev. Lett. 86:2790, 2001, Sec. 2, around Eq. 2.11) [V as to that phrase; equation not worked through].
2.2 Steady Eulerian density: d/dt=0 gives div(rho u) = 0. In 1D, or along a channel of fixed cross-section, rho = C/u(x) pointwise (flux conservation), C fixed by total mass. In 2D/3D with div u != 0 there is no pointwise solution in general: integrate along streamlines or solve the PDE; with sinks, the steady state concentrates on the sinks [M, standard continuity reasoning; not sourced this pass].
2.3 -div u is the instantaneous compression rate, d(ln rho)/dt = -div u at the moving particle: a PW rate, not a density [M, follows from 2.1].
2.4 Inertial particles in an incompressible flow: to first order in the Stokes time tau, v = u - tau Du/Dt is compressible, div v = -tau tr(A^2) with A = grad u. PW given A (and Du/Dt). Heavy particles leave high-vorticity regions and collect in low-vorticity/high-strain regions; light particles the opposite. Read: in compressible fields preferential concentration occurs where field divergence is negative; in incompressible fields where strain is high and skewness negative; Eq. 25 defines |S|^2 and |omega|^2 and notes that the average of d_a u_b d_b u_a = |S|^2 - |omega|^2/2 vanishes for incompressible fields [V] "Preferential concentration vs. clustering in inertial particle transport by random velocity fields", arXiv 0811.2927, Secs. II, IV (Eqs. 21-27), V. The explicit div v = -tau tr(A^2) is the standard form given in a search summary of Balkovsky et al.; prefactors unchecked [S/M]. Maxey 1987, J. Fluid Mech. 174:441-465, relates the particle-flow compressibility to local straining of the carrier (read through abstract and a secondary account only; the "preferential sweeping" naming is Wang and Maxey 1993) [S]. Validity: weak inertia; the clustering is intermittent with heavy-tailed fluctuations [V] BFF abstract.
2.5 Clustering of inertial particles can also occur in incompressible flow ("clumps and voids") [V] arXiv 0811.2927 Sec. IV.
2.6 Failure modes of the divergence proxy [M]: -div u is a rate, not a density; in a steady flow with sinks the exponential integrates to a singularity at the sink; in an incompressible flow (div u = 0) a uniform passive-tracer number density stays uniform, so no pointwise convergence signal exists and patchiness comes only from the tracer's own initial inhomogeneity or from inertia/buoyancy/swimming.

## 3. Gravity-slope (topographic) habitat suitability and game density functions

3.1 Seamount/topography aggregation mechanisms in the literature: enhanced prey flux past topography and interception of vertical migrators (topographic blockage); higher abundance at crests tied to enhanced current amplitudes and convergence zones (Hawaii study) [S] https://deepsea.uac.pt/doi-2901 and linked results. Slope is one covariate among depth, rugosity, aspect, BPI, VRM in Maxent studies; depth was usually the best predictor and slope the weakest in a Western Pacific snapper study [S] agris.fao.org record 671224ca7f591113e2a47f06. Suitability in those is a fitted function; no source found that derives a niche from gradient magnitude alone, and none for shelf-break or upwelling covariates in seamount models [S].
3.2 Horizon Zero Dawn (Guerrilla, GDC 2017, J. van Muijden): placement is "data driven, deterministic, locally stable"; per-layer DENSITYMAP step evaluates artist logic over mipmapped world-data maps (heights, Erosion_Flow/Wear/Deposition, Water_Flow, Water_Vorticity, Terrain_Cavity, painted Placement_* maps); GENERATE discretises the density map by dithering against a precomputed pattern ("even spread thresholds, maximize 2D distance"), one sample per thread; layered dithering with a two-sided threshold test avoids inter-layer collision readback; about 250 microseconds average GPU load, 500+ asset types, 100,000+ objects; regenerated at run time around the player [V] https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf (slides read, 238 MB PDF). The density is a continuous scalar combined from baked maps; the spatial pattern comes from the fixed threshold pattern. Inputs are baked 2D maps, not analytic pointwise functions.
3.3 Unreal PCG: "Normal To Density" turns the angle between a point's normal and a reference vector into density with Result = Result^(1/Strength) and an Offset; "Density Filter" keeps points whose density is in [LowerBound, UpperBound] (0-1) with optional remap; "Density Noise" modulates density by bounded noise (Density = OriginalDensity op DensityNoise); Surface Sampler can keep zero-density points; seed controls repeatability [S] https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PCG/UPCGNormalToDensitySettings , .../UPCGDensityFilterSettings , .../UPCGSpatialNoiseSettings .
3.4 Houdini: Scatter SOP "Density Attribute" varies point density smoothly across a primitive from a point or vertex attribute; Relax Iterations gives even spacing (turn off for random look); LOP Scatter Instances has an Up Axis mask with Max Angle [S] https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/lop/scatterinstances.html and forum posts https://www.sidefx.com/forum/post/178860/ .
3.5 Sources of the "nice patterns": in HZD, painted/generated maps plus the fixed blue-noise-like threshold pattern [V]; in Unreal/Houdini, a density attribute plus relaxation or density noise [S]. No Man's Sky (GDC 2017 talks) not opened this pass [M, unverified]. No source found for a gradient/curvature-to-pattern recipe.

## 4. Determinism and stability under motion and field change

4.1 Fixed per-point threshold with field-dependent density: HZD's dither-based discretisation compares the density value to a fixed pattern value; each candidate's threshold is constant, so the set at lower density is a subset of the set at higher density (monotone nesting) and a point appears or disappears only when the density at it crosses its own threshold [V for the dither design; the nesting consequence is my reading, M]. "Locally stable" is in the slide's own goal list [V].
4.2 Independently generated sets per density level are not nested and change abruptly between levels; one fix is a hierarchy where denser levels grow out from sparser ones [S] 3D-TSV, arXiv 2112.09202 (as found in search; page not read).
4.3 Blue-noise candidates: Wei, "Parallel Poisson disk sampling", ACM TOG 27(3), SIGGRAPH 2008: grid cells, concurrent sampling of non-conflicting cells, adaptive sampling from an importance field, constant passes for a target count [S]. Kopf, Cohen, Deussen, Lischinski, "Recursive Wang Tiles for Real-Time Blue Noise", ACM TOG 25(3):509-518 (2006) (authorship per search; not Lagae-Dutre): tile-based blue noise with spatially varying density; stability by position-indexed tiles is [M]. Ahmed, Niese, Huang, Deussen, "An adaptive point sampler on a regular lattice", ACM TOG 36(4) (2017): one sample per lattice tile, word-based tile IDs, bit-reversal traversal of sub-tiles for varying density [S]. Ulichney void-and-cluster (1993) and Mitchell best-candidate (1991) are the classic ordered-threshold constructions [M]. Hash-per-cell jittered grids are the simplest deterministic, position-stable placement but are not blue noise [M].
4.4 Spatiotemporal blue noise masks (Wolfe et al., EGSR 2022): values well distributed over time, blue spectra in space and time, built by reformulating the void-and-cluster energy function [V] https://www.ea.com/seed/news/egsr-2022-blue-noise (page read; paper PDF not readable by the fetcher). Aimed at TAA and Monte Carlo, not point placement.
4.5 Render-side anti-popping: dithered LOD cross-fade with the opacity-mask dither threshold ramped over about 0.25-0.5 s (Cesium for Unreal) [S] https://cesium.com/blog/2022/10/20/smoother-lod-transitions-in-cesium-for-unreal/ ; hysteresis offset added to LOD distance to avoid flicker at a boundary (Blender BGE commit, default 10%) [S] https://demo.gitea.com/kannonier8/blender/src/commit/e7d051043dc82c2991eb491e0630d9b1065934c3/release . Side effect: a half-faded LOD still casts shadows/occlusion [S].
4.6 Time-varying field [M, reasoning; no source found on temporal coherence of density-threshold placement]: with continuous d(x,t) and fixed thresholds, appearance is continuous at the rate of change of d; a fast field (the earlier probe measured flow speed comparable to rail speed, 6.06 vs 6.28 u/s) still moves the set at that rate. Hysteresis needs separate enter/exit thresholds.

## 5. Predator-prey arrangement as a closed form

5.1 Ideal free distribution (Fretwell and Lucas 1970, Acta Biotheoretica 19:16-36 [M]): payoff equal and maximal in all occupied patches. With input rates r_i, the fraction in patch i is p_i = r_i / (r_1 + ... + r_n) (Parker's input-matching rule). PW given the resource field plus a global sum for normalisation [S] https://fix.prf.jcu.cz/~krivan/clanky/KrivanCressmanSchneider-TPB-2008.pdf (Krivan, Cressman, Schneider, Theor. Popul. Biol. 73 (2008); PDF fetched, formula seen only in search snippets).
5.2 Predator distribution proportional to prey: x_i = P R_i / sum R_j; with interference exponent m in (0,1), predator density scales as prey density to the power 1/m; the proportional case is globally asymptotically stable under replicator dynamics; input matching is often imperfect, with poor patches over-used because of interference [S] Tsybulin and Zelenchuk 2024, Mathematics 12(2):275 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jmathe/v12y2024i2p275-d1319317.html ), https://dmorris.lakeheadu.ca/29.pdf .
5.3 Two-trophic-level IFD (both species move): equilibria depend on the functional response; Holling type II destabilises the spatial distribution; some equilibria put all predators in one patch; the theory is developed for two-patch systems [V as to existence of these statements in the Krivan et al. 2008 PDF text, Sec. 3.2 and Fig. 5, details partial]. No continuous-space closed form for both species found [S].
5.4 Tritrophic IFD test (lady beetles, aphids, beans): predators aggregated in the highest-quality patch as predicted; prey distribution near uniform [S] https://czaw.org/?p=1905 .
5.5 If the prey density is itself a pointwise field (Secs. 1-3), the IFD predator field composes pointwise, with the global sum if totals are held fixed [M, composition].

## Pointwise versus path-integrated
PW: Okubo-Weiss W (and Q, lambda2); instantaneous divergence -div u; largest strain eigenvalue (T->0 FTLE); inertial-particle compressibility -tau tr(A^2); slope/curvature of the gravity field; density = f(local quantities) with a fixed per-point threshold; ideal free distribution given a prior field (plus a global sum when totals are fixed).
PI: FTLE and FSLE; density from exp(-integral of div u dt) along a trajectory; steady-state Eulerian density in 2D/3D compressible flow; LCS ridge extraction; Langmuir windrow position unless the roll field is analytic (then PW).
