**"Check the sky, then your radiators, then the slope. Then you know where you are."**

> Draft lore pass r3, 2026-10-14, for the operator's review. Part of [[Spaceborne Fauna]]. Pseudospace is not one sea. It comes in flavours, some wildly different, and a pilot learns to taste the change. This note gives the grammar of a flavour, four flavours, and the tells for each. [[Adrasteia Space]] is the first flavour developed in full. `[new]` marks invention that touches existing canon. The reasoning and the changes argued for are in [[Fauna Lore Review r3]].

Canon already says the frontier is wild. [[Pseudospace]] is space whose geometry does not follow Euclid, and beyond the islands of flat space "absolutely anything is possible". Anything-is-possible is true and unplayable: a pilot can't learn it. Flavours are the playable cut. Each flavour is a reliable set of rules a pilot can learn, cross out of, and come back to. The places nobody has learned yet stay unnamed.

#### What a Flavour Is

The [[Aether]] is the same field everywhere, but canon says what it does "varies with local conditions". A flavour is the Aether's local temper: a fixed answer to five questions [new].

| Dial | The question | Why it matters to a pilot |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | How far does light carry, and what eats it? | What you can see, and what can see you. |
| **Heat** | Where does a ship's heat go? | Wild [[Negative Entropy]] turns a little heat into light everywhere. Whether that light escapes, is eaten, or never forms decides whether radiators work. |
| **Surface** | Does the slope hold its shape? | A solid slope can be charted. A liquid one heaves, and every chart goes out of date. |
| **Flow** | What moves the medium, and from where? | A braided current, pulses from a source, nothing, or something stranger. Everything that does not swim goes where the flow takes it. |
| **Distance** | Does a kilometre stay a kilometre? | Where the scale bends, ranges lie, sight lines curve, and two far places can touch. |

The stars set the temper. A region's flavour is the character of its stars and how they sit in the Aether, which is why the flavours have borders and do not blend into soup: a region belongs to its stars as a watershed belongs to its rivers [new]. Whether the builders of the Aether placed those stars, and so chose the flavours, is left open (see "Are they the garden?" in [[Fauna Lore Review r2]]).

Life follows the temper. Each flavour has its own energy budget, so each has its own bestiary. Very little crosses from one to another, because what feeds an animal in one flavour does not exist in the next.

#### Four Flavours

**1. Open Water (the common family).** Starlit and clear. The nebula glows because [[Glim]] lives in it, the slope is solid, a braided current runs through every zone, and heat leaks out as light any warm thing can be seen by. This is the flavour [[Pseudospace Ecology]] describes, and it is a family rather than one place: from the **Shallows** near flat space, where the current is thin and the animals small, to the **Deeps** out toward the Chaos factions, where the current is strong, the glim thick and the animals huge. Most of the map is Open Water.

**2. The Ink ([[Adrasteia Space]]).** A light-eating fog closes overhead and all around, and because the fog eats the light a warm thing gives off, it drinks heat too: radiators work too well. The slope is liquid. Stars are strung on pillars of glowing nebula that breathe out waves of stardust, and the pressure of each wave opens travelling pockets of clear water. The dust carries strange matter, and the animals that live in the inky clouds concentrate it. Every Ink anyone has found, Adrasteia was already in [new].

**3. The Glass.** The Aether here is stiff. There is no current. The slope is still enough to be a mirror, the nebula hangs where it has always hung, and light carries further than anywhere else: you can see everything in a Glass zone, and everything can see you. Heat has nowhere to go, because the Aether makes no light of it: ships overheat, and every wake a hull cuts stays printed on the slope for hours. The few animals are slow, enormous and mostly still, grown over centuries in water that never moves. It is the opposite of the Ink: there you cannot see; here you cannot hide [new].

**4. The Fold.** Here the distance scale bends. Near a **knot** a kilometre grows longer the deeper in you go, so a moon's neighbourhood is bigger inside than it looks from outside, and light and ships alike follow curves through it. Along a **seam** two far places are glued edge to edge, and flying through the seam puts you in the other one. This is pseudospace being what canon says it is: not Euclidean. The flavour is detailed below because it has no bestiary yet and its interest is how it flies [new].

Sol and the other islands of flat space are not a flavour. They are the absence of one: no current, no glim, no food, and a distance that never bends.

#### How a Pilot Knows They Have Crossed

| Into | The sky | Thermal | The slope and the flow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open Water | Stars and glowing nebula | Warm things glow; radiators normal | Solid slope; a steady braided current |
| The Ink | The stars go out. Fog closes overhead, then all around | Radiators work too well; your own hull is the brightest thing on the board | The slope heaves. A slow swell arrives on a beat |
| The Glass | Everything is visible, very far away, very sharp | Radiators stall; heat climbs | The current stops. Your wake does not fade |
| The Fold | Stars double, smear or sit in the wrong place | Normal | Ranges stop agreeing with each other; a straight course curves |

Crossing is not instant. Flavours meet at a **margin** a few minutes' flight wide where the tells arrive one at a time, and where the borderland animals of both sides mix. An Ink margin is a grey band where the stars dim before they vanish; a Glass margin is where the current slackens and dies.

#### The Fold, Flown

Nothing is hidden in the Fold and nothing is where it looks.

- **Sight lines bend.** Light curves around a knot, so you can see a contact that no straight shot can reach, and see it from two directions at once. A gunner learns to fire along the curve, not at the image. Beams follow the light and bend with it; slugs and missiles follow their own course, which bends differently. Learning the difference is learning to fight here [new].
- **Ranges lie, but honestly.** A target that reads 2,000 out across a knot may be four times that to fly. Range on sensors is the light's path, which is the truth about the light and not about your trip. Pilots fly by **depth**, how deep into a knot they are, not by distance [new].
- **Inside is bigger.** A knot can hold a whole battle inside a ring that looks a minute wide. Fleets hide in them. A pursuer who chases you into a knot finds the space unfolding ahead faster than either of you can cross it.
- **Seams.** A seam is a doorway with no door. Through one you are somewhere far away; look back through it and you see where you came from, small and close. Seams make ambushes possible from places that are not nearby, and make retreat a puzzle: the way out may not be the way in. Unlike a wormhole mouth, a seam is not a hole in space; it is two edges of space that happen to be the same edge.
- **You meet yourself.** Light can go round a knot and come back, so a ship deep in a Fold sometimes sees its own image ahead of it, minutes old. Every spacer story about a ghost ship that matches your hull ends with a pilot realizing the ghost turned when she did. The image is real light, not a lie the Fold tells: it is where you were.

Spacers say *"never trust a straight line in the Fold"* and *"if it matches you, it is you"*.
