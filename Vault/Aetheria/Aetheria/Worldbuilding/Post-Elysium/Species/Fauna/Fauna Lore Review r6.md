#### Where I'd Change Your Framing

**1. A pillar does everything a sun does except shine on its zone.** Taken whole, "instead of regular suns" would give the Ink daylight, and the fog of your own sketch forbids it: light dies within a short distance. So I made the pillar a sun turned inside out. Its light goes up the column and leaves; its warmth goes out across the surface as dust, a beat at a time. It is still the reach's sun in mass, in well, in being what everything orbits, and in being the only warmth for a long way, which is all your sentence needs. What changes is what the ecology is organised around. It is not light and not steady heat. It is the **beat**: warmth delivered in pulses, so that every living thing in the Ink keeps time with its pillar the way Earth's shore animals keep a tidal clock. That answers the question of what the Ink carries instead of light, and it is the most useful thing in this round, because it gives every Ink animal the same tell (they move on the pulse) and gives a player one thing to learn first.

**2. "Cradling" should be a band at arm's length, not an embrace.** A star cradles life at a distance: too close is lethal, too far is frozen. I gave the pillar the same shape. The eye is still and bright and lifeless, because light is no food there and every beat scours it; the break at its rim is where the waves come up, steep and hot, and only cachalots stay there; the cradle between the break and the bank is where life is; past the bank is the dark. This keeps your "cradling" and makes it mean something a pilot can feel. It also matches what the placement law already says (`fauna-placement-story`: density is zero in well cores).

**3. Your "inky clouds" are a ring, not a cloud at the pillar's foot.** r5 had them densest near the pillars. With a well under the Ink, each wave lifts dust outward and uphill and the dust slides back between waves; it settles where the waves give out. That is a ring at the outer edge of the cradle: the **bank**, the Ink's frost line, where what the pillar throws out finally stops. Squid lair there and cachalots dive after them. The richest dust stays where r5 put it, at the foot, which is now under the water.

**4. "Pillars" in a zone: one, and rarely two.** Your sentence is plural. I read it as one pillar per zone, as with suns, plus rare **pairs**, where two wells run together, two sets of waves cross in a chop, and the dust of both reaches piles up along the seam. Pairs are the richest and hardest water in the Ink. That is question 2.

#### Where I'd Change r5

**1. r5 said the Ink's stars "are not suns". That is reversed.** The stars strung up the column are not suns, any of them; the pillar they make together is.

**2. The anchors move from high in the column to the eye at the bottom of the well.** r5 pictured a tall column with the anchors high up and the foot at the surface. In the game a pillar is a zone's central body, a point at the zone's centre with a well around it, and "high" has nowhere to be. The eye does the same work and comes from the gravity law itself: inside a body's radius the pull falls to zero at the centre (`softened-core`), so the still water an anchor needs is the bottom of the well. In flatspace r5 already had anchors at "a well's slack point"; now both spaces hold anchors where the pulls cancel.

**3. The foot goes under the water, and leaving the eye means crossing the break between beats.** r5 had nobody reach the foot. A ship coming out of an eye must cross the eye's rim to go anywhere, so the rim cannot be the forbidden place. The foot is now below the surface, under the break, and still unreached; the break is crossed between beats, which is when the gates already open. A player's first act in the Ink is timing a crossing to the pulse.

**4. Inky clouds, squid, cachalots and grey move to the bank and the break** (Framing 3).

**5. Two rulings that landed after r5 was committed are now applied.** r5 left `why-flatspace-is-flat` open and gave blind gates no aim; `flatspace-held-by-minds-in-timeless-elysium` and `blind-gate-targeting` were admitted minutes after the r5 commit. [[Flatspace]] now says flatspace is held by minds and the Timeless built that into Elysium, and the three old accounts survive as politics, each partly right. [[Anchors and Gates]] now says an anchor is held the same way, and that aim was learned first for nearby flatspace and barely exists into Wonderland. Your ruling ("the hidden downside is so does every other thing that ever got confined to this place, and some of them come from very strange places") suggests that the layers of Wonderland are other prisoners' realities. I put that in as something spacers think, not a fact; question 4 asks whether to make it one.

#### For the Game, Not the Lore

#### (3) Open Questions

1. **Do pillars stand in flatspace?** Your ruling left it open. *My recommendation: no natural pillar stands in flatspace.* Flatspace is where minds hold Euclid; a pillar is the Ink's law, and one standing in the open would also give away that the Ink exists somewhere a telescope could follow. A pillar may be glimpsed through a tear, as the rarest of tells. Adrasteia's flatspace gate rings stay ordinary rings.
2. **May an Ink zone have two pillars?** *My recommendation: yes, rarely, as pairs.* The gravity law already superposes wells and the Skein already draws the seam between them, so a pair costs no new engine, and it gives the Ink a rare rich zone that is hard to fly. If you want every Ink zone to read as one sun, cut one paragraph in [[Adrasteia Space]].
3. **Do ships feel the swell?** The lore has ships riding waves out and fighting them in, while `flow-moves-what-ruled` says ships do not drift with the flow. *My recommendation: yes. The swell is not the flow; it is a short radial push on each beat, felt by ships as well as loose matter.* That is what makes the beat playable: timing the break, riding out, struggling in. It is a design fork for a later cut; until it is ruled, the lore stays as written.
4. **Is the Ink something else's reality, held the way we hold flatspace?** Your flatness ruling makes it possible: everything confined in Elysium imposes its reality, and some of it comes from very strange places. *My recommendation: keep it a spacer theory and never confirm it.* It would explain too much at once (why only the eye holds an anchor, what the stars are, who supplies Adrasteia), and canon wants the Ink's nature kept dark. #### Rulings After r6

**Where I'd change your sketch.**

1. **A still summit, not a singularity.** A true singularity pushes without limit at its centre, so nothing could sit in the column. Every gate into the Ink opens there, and the keepers live there. I kept the push finite and let it cancel at the very centre, which gives a balance point like a ball on a hilltop: anything set down stays, and anything nudged off is thrown down the fall. Arrival then reads well: the pilot lets go of the anchor and the pillar's push takes the ship. In both spaces anchors still hold where forces cancel.
2. **Life is on the outer wall, not at the bottom of the bowl.** Read literally, "cradle" puts life on the floor of the bowl. But the floor is where the dust and the dead settle, and the swell moves life up the wall: a pocket is fastest on the fall and slowest as it climbs the outer wall, so warm water lingers there longest. The floor belongs to the bank and the squid.
3. **Past the outer wall the ground levels off.** A tin has a rim and nothing beyond it. I let the push give out so the ground settles level, which makes the dark a beatless flat between reaches. It also means pairs merge their bowls instead of meeting at a ridge.

**New questions.**

1. **Is the centre a still summit or a true singularity?** *My recommendation: a still summit.* It keeps a place for anchors, gates and keepers in the eye, and keeps one rule for anchors in both spaces (they hold where forces cancel). A true singularity would push the anchors out to the bowl floor, among the animals.
2. **What lies past a bowl's outer wall?** *My recommendation: level ground, the dark, with no beat.* The alternative is ground that keeps rising into ridges between reaches. Ridges would make the slow way a climb over a watershed and give pairs a ridge instead of a shared trough; that is dramatic, but it adds a second slope the Ink's gravity law would have to carry.

#### The Pillar Is a Wall

Ruling `pillar-is-a-wall` corrects the pillar's scale, in your words: "this spike should be as big around as a large sun currently is, an impassable wall of nebulae glowing and frothing with dark energy, maybe it does summit but it does so so high up that you'll never see it, much less get anywhere close even with the strongest thrusters in the game. It presents up close as a vertical wall of gravity you have no hope of climbing". You also said: "You can indeed do sick bounce maneuvers off of a big negative mass like this". This overturns the still summit above and settles the first of the two questions just above. The lore was swept again in place.

**What changed.**

- **[[Adrasteia Space]]**: nothing stands on or in a pillar.
  - **The wall.** The pillar fills the zone's centre like a large sun's disc. From the bowl it is a glow behind the fog that fills half the sky. Up close it is a face of frothing nebula with moving dark lanes, and the push rises from slope to vertical in the last stretch, the **foot**, where thrusters at full only hold a ship a while before it is thrown back.
  - **The waves come from the face.** On each beat the whole face heaves at once and throws a swell off all the way round, carrying dust frothed off it. The richest dust is now the froth on the face, not a foot under the water. The swell runs down the foot, sweeps the floor, and climbs and dies on the outer slope, as before.
  - **Anchors, gates and keepers live on the floor of the bowl, in the bank.** The arrival scene now opens in the bank's dark under the glow. Keepers live in cold stations among the squid; it is Adrasteia's most dangerous posting and its most honoured.
  - **A new section, Meeting the Wall:** the **bounce** (the foot hands a ship's speed back at the matching slant, for free; pursuit ends at the wall), the **kiss** (opening the scoops at the top of a bounce for the froth; on the beat the face's heave is added and the ship is thrown), and timing every bounce to the count. Spacers say *kiss the wall, don't lean on it*. **Wall-kissers** join Who Goes In.
  - Bands are now face, foot, bank, cradle, dark. The old charts' picture of stars strung up a column survives only as what Adrasteia draws inside the wall.
- **[[Cachalots]]**: they skim the froth at the foot, bounce off the wall without seeming to try, and dive into the bank. **[[Soot]]** and **[[Rays]]** are reworded from "outer wall" to "outer slope" so that "wall" means the pillar.
- **[[Anchors and Gates]]**: the Ink's anchors hold on the floor of a pillar's bowl and creep round it. A pillar glimpsed through a tear is now a wall of frothing light. **[[Wonderland]]**: the Ink's gates sit in bowls.

**The anchor's new home, and what it does.** The floor of the bowl is the right home, and the rule improves on it: anchors hold where forces cancel *and push back*. The floor is a valley, so an anchor nudged off it slides home. The old summit was a hilltop, which needed lore to explain why anything stayed there. Moving the anchors there does four things to the lore:

1. **Gates open among the animals, in the darkest place in the reach.** Arrival is now a held breath in the bank, not a lamp. Keepers have to run cold to live, which ties Adrasteia's second lock (the Ink cannot be worked warm) to its first (only they hold anchors).
2. **Anchors creep.** A valley that runs round the pillar holds an anchor in one direction only, so it creeps along the floor. I kept this: an Ink anchor is "never where it was planted, only how far round", keepers follow their anchors, and an anchor's tuning carries its creep. That is question 1.
3. **The monopoly loosens in principle.** Any floor of any pillar can now hold an anchor, so a rival's lucky blind gate that came up on a floor could be anchored by a crew that survived the bank. None is known to have done it. I kept that: a rumoured rival anchor in some bank is a better story than a law of nature saying it cannot be done.
4. **The richest dust moves from an unreachable foot to a reachable but deadly face.** The froth is taken by kissing the wall, which is a craft and not a secret. This gives you the bounce play you described and an economic reason to master it.

**Where I'd change your framing.** One place only. "A vertical wall of gravity you have no hope of climbing" should still have a **foot**: a short stretch where the slope steepens from gentle to vertical, instead of a cliff with no lead-in. A bounce needs that stretch to read as a craft, because that is where the pilot feels the push building and chooses the slant. A true cliff would make every contact the same collision.

**New questions.**

1. **Do Ink anchors creep round the pillar?** *My recommendation: yes.* Creep is what a ring-shaped valley does, and it gives keepers a job and gates a phase. The alternative is to hold anchors only where the field cancels in every direction. In a single reach no such place exists, so every gate into the Ink would have to open in the narrows of a pair, and pairs are rare.
2. **What does the wall give back?** *My recommendation: exactly what a ship brings between beats, a clean mirror at the matching slant; and on the beat the face's heave is added on top.* That makes bouncing free and predictable most of the time and makes the count the whole skill of the kiss. It is for the Ink gravity law (`aetheria-release:follow_up:ink-gravity-law`).
