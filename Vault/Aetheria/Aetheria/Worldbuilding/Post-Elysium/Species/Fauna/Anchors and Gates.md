**"Anyone can open a gate. Getting it to open somewhere is the expensive part."**

> Draft lore pass r5, 2026-10-14, for the operator's review. Part of [[Spaceborne Fauna]], beside [[Flatspace]] and [[Wonderland]]. Built on two operator rulings: a gate needs an anchor at its far end, or it lands with very little control and usually somewhere useless (`gates-need-anchors`); most links are gates someone paid for, and a few natural tears exist as rare, dangerous wonders (`mostly-gates-few-tears`). `[new]` marks invention that touches existing canon. Reasoning in [[Fauna Lore Review r5]].

Humans learned to open gates long before they understood anything about what the gates open into. Every wormhole on the map is a gate someone paid for, and the far end of every one of them was nailed down, at a price, by someone who went there first. That is how Elysium is colonised, flatspace included. A **link** is any way from one space to another; nearly all of them are gates, and the rest are tears.

#### Gates

A gate is a ring of machinery that opens a mouth in one place and a second mouth somewhere else. Opening one costs what a station costs, and holding an unanchored gate open costs a fortune by the hour [new].

What the ring cannot choose is where the far mouth goes. Left to itself, the far mouth falls wherever the [[Aether]] takes it, and the Aether has no interest in where you meant to go. To open a gate *somewhere*, the far end needs an **anchor**.

#### Anchors

An anchor is an instrument with a twin. The pair is cultivated together, the way the [[Wavecrafters]] cultivate any resonant element, until each has learned its relation to the other exactly; [[The Glass]], where nothing fades, is where most are grown. One twin stays in the ring. The other goes out and is planted. Open the ring on its twin and the far mouth falls toward the anchor, and stays there cheaply for as long as both are kept [new].

That makes anchors, not gates, the asset. A ring can be built anywhere by anyone with the money. An anchor has to be *carried* to the far end, by a crew, without a gate, because there is no gate there yet. **Whoever holds the anchors holds the map.** Anchor registries are the deeds of Elysium; anchor keepers are its landlords; an anchor's tuning is the most closely held fact a company owns. Undertow retuners claim they can teach an anchor a second twin. Registries call that piracy. The Undertow calls it a second opinion [new].

Anchors drift, wear and go quiet, and an anchor nobody keeps is a door slowly closing. Every anchored gate has someone living at its far end.

#### The Slow Way

There are two ways to put an anchor where you want it, and only one of them is reliable [new].

**The slow way** is to go there without a gate. In flatspace that means crossing the dark between islands by drive: months, years, a crew that leaves young. In a layer of [[Wonderland]] it means getting in by any link at all (a tear, a rival's gate, someone else's lucky blind stab), then crossing the layer itself to the place you want, with no road home but the one you are carrying. Plant the anchor, and someone can open a gate onto it. The crew comes home through the gate they made possible, if they come home.

This is the fae-legend journey: the walk under the hill, the long road through a country with its own law, the thing you must carry and must not set down. The slow way is how every known place in Elysium was first made reachable, and the crews who did it are the founding saints of every company that holds a lane.

#### The Blind Gate

**The blind gate** is the other way: open a ring with no anchor and see what is on the other side. It is the biggest bet in Elysium. Canon's corporations that go "all-in with the project of colonizing and exploiting Wonderland" are the ones who place it [new].

| What is through | How often | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing useful: dark, empty, a hundred years from anywhere, or the rim of [[Hyperspace]] | Almost always | The meter runs, the ring is shut, the money is gone |
| Somewhere that looks back | Rarely | The ring is shut fast, if it can be. Not everything that comes through is reported |
| Somewhere worth having: a rich ground, a new layer | Very rarely | A race. An unanchored mouth drifts, so a crew must go through and plant an anchor before the gate closes or a rival's crew does it first |

A jackpot blind gate turns into the slow way at once: the crew who go through it to plant the anchor are on the long road, with the ring's meter running behind them. Blind-gate syndicates sell shares in a stake, insurers will not touch one, and the tales of gates that came up rich are told everywhere and are mostly about other people.

Spacers say the first blind gate was the one that brought us here: the [[FTL Trigger]] was humanity's first stab into the seething void, and it landed on Elysium [new].

#### Tears

A few links nobody built. **Tears** are places where two spaces have come apart and touch, with no ring at either end and no anchor anyone planted. They are rare, they move or keep hours, they leak their far side (the stars dim, the current dies, the stars double), and animals know them long before people do. Following animals is still the oldest way to find one [new].

A tear is a wonder and a danger in the same breath. It goes somewhere no blind gate has been, it costs nothing to open, and nothing on the far side was expecting company either. Some tears are older than humanity's arrival, and some spacers swear a few of them are not tears at all, but gates the other prisoners of Elysium built and stopped keeping. Powers beyond comprehension wait past them, and treasures we should have left alone [new].

#### Where Anchors Hold

Every space treats an anchor differently, and that, more than anything, decides who holds it [new].

| Space | Where an anchor holds | Can a rival find it? | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Flatspace]] | Anywhere still: a well's slack point, a moon, a station | Yes, with work | Everyone; companies own lanes |
| [[Adrasteia Space\|The Ink]] | Only in a pillar's clear column, the one still water | No; no light leaves | Adrasteia, every known link |
| [[The Glass]] | Anywhere; the stiff Aether holds everything | At once; nothing hides | The Wavecrafters, by watching, not hiding |
| [[The Fold]] | Anywhere, but it is in two places, and a gate may open at either | Yes, from both sides | Nobody |
| [[Hyperspace]] | Anywhere, and you will never find it again without beacons | Only by the chain | The beacon keepers |

#### Why an Anchor Works

Nobody knows why a twin pulls a gate's far mouth toward it. Each account of [[Flatspace#Why Flatspace Is Flat|why flatspace is flat]] has its own answer: the core worlds call it engineering; the Chaos factions say an anchor is an expectation made solid, a piece of somewhere that someone insists on; the Timeless account says anchors are scratches on the inside of the cell wall, and the Aether lets them stand because it lets everything stand that keeps the prisoners busy [new].

Spacers say: *no anchor, no door*; *a ring is a promise, an anchor is a deed*; and, of anyone who talks up a blind gate, *show me who walked there*.
