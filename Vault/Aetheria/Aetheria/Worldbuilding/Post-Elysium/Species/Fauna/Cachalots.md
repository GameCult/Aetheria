**"Nobody hunts cachalots. People follow them, and pick up the grey."**

> Draft r3, 2026-10-14. Part of [[Adrasteia Space]] (the Ink); niche: apex. Named for the sperm whale, the Earth animal that dives into the dark after giant squid. Not related to the [[Whales]] of Open Water, and spacers who confuse them don't do it twice. `[new]` marks invention that touches existing canon.

The largest thing in the Ink: a blunt, scarred head, a body many times a ship's length, skin the colour of the fog. Cachalots live near the pillars, deep in the inky clouds, and rise into a pocket only to take the warmth, or to kill.

**Eats.** [[Squid]], and the dust they carry. Every squid a cachalot eats adds its dust to the cachalot's. That is where the Ink's dust ends up: at the top of the web, in a few hundred animals near each pillar [new].

**Moves: the tell.** Long, deep dives into the inky clouds, out of every sensor's reach, and slow rises into a pocket near the pillar's foot. They stay close to the pillars and ride no waves. When a cachalot rises, the squid in that pocket scatter.

**What a pilot learns.** A cachalot does not need to see you. It holds so much dust that it eats heat at a range no other animal can, and it hunts squid by their heat in the inky clouds, where nothing can see anything. To a cachalot a ship is a very warm squid.

**On sensors.** Usually nothing: it is in the clouds. When it rises, a cold so large the board reads a hole. Then, if it feeds, the flare.

**How it hurts.** *The pull*: heat drained from a ship at long range, faster than any squid can manage. *The lance*: a cachalot that has eaten more heat than it can hold throws it off as a beam of hard light, the negent conversion done all at once. It is the brightest thing in the Ink after a pillar, and it is what spacers mean when they say a ship was *lit* [new]. *The ram*, for anything that has made it angry.

**Yield.** *Grey*: like the ambergris of Earth's sperm whales, a waxy concretion cachalots pass, built around the indigestible beaks of the squid they eat and dense with dust. It drifts up into pockets near the pillars, and grey-pickers trail cachalots for weeks to find it. Grey holds more dust than anything else a living crew can take. That a cachalot's body holds more still is common knowledge. Nobody sells any [new].

**Lives.** The inky clouds at the foot of each pillar, where the dust is thickest and every wave begins. They are why nobody reaches the foot of a pillar.

*Designers:* a rare anchor near pillars that rises into pockets on a long cycle; a very long-range heat drain; a heat-threshold beam attack; a grey drop that can be collected without a kill. Treat a kill as out of reach unless a later ruling makes it a quest.
