#### Where I'd Change Your Decisions

**1. "Very little control" should still leave a bias.** If a blind gate lands uniformly anywhere, nobody can learn anything from one, and the bet is a lottery ticket. I gave it one bias with a physical reason: hyperspace has more room than everything else together, so an unanchored mouth is likeliest to fall there, onto its rim. That makes "usually somewhere useless" a fact about geometry, gives the Lost a second origin (blind-gate crews who went through to look), and ties your "blind stab into the seething void" to a specific void. If you want blind gates to be completely unpredictable, delete one bullet in [[Hyperspace]] and one table cell in [[Anchors and Gates]].

**2. A blind gate should be a race, not a single coin toss.** Your ruling has the expense wasted when the gate lands badly. I added that an unanchored far mouth drifts and costs a fortune by the hour to hold open. So a gate that comes up rich is not yet won: a crew must go through and plant an anchor before the mouth drifts away or a rival's crew does it first. That joins your two ways of colonising into one loop, since every jackpot turns into the slow way with a meter running, and it gives the jackpot a playable shape.

**3. "Adrasteia pillars are a regular level feature": I read it as every zone of the Ink being a pillar's reach**, so a pillar is the Ink's ordinary landmark, not a set piece. If you meant that Adrasteian pillars also stand in flatspace levels, for example as the home rings of their gates, that is one paragraph in [[Adrasteia Space]] and fits the frame.

#### Where I'd Change Self's Readings

**1. The pillars hold the anchors; they are not anchors themselves.** An anchor is half of a cultivated twin. A pillar is a natural thing the stars make, and if it were an anchor, anyone's ring could open on it. What a pillar gives is the only still water in the Ink: the fog moves and the surface heaves, so an anchor holds only inside a pillar's clear column. That makes the monopoly what Self wanted, property won by the slow, deadly walk, and visible on the field: Adrasteian anchor-lights in every column. It also makes rival blind gates into the Ink fail for a reason, since they open in the fog, where nothing holds.

**2. "Who holds anchors holds the map" works differently in each space, and that decides each foothold.** I kept Self's line and made it a table: in flatspace anchors hold anywhere and can be found, so companies compete for lanes; in the Ink they hold only in pillars and cannot be found, so Adrasteia holds everything known; in the Glass they hold anywhere and cannot be hidden, so the Wavecrafters hold the layer by watching; in the Fold an anchor is in two places, so nobody holds it; in hyperspace you cannot find one again without beacons, so the keepers hold it. One mechanism now explains all four footholds, replacing r4's separate reasons.

**3. Beacons and anchors: one instrument, not only one industry.** A beacon is an anchor that also chimes. Both are cultivated twins, and canon's Sacred Instruments, grown where "every disturbance stays", are the natural thing to make them from, so the Glass is where Elysium's anchors are grown. Self's other readings (two expedition kinds, lanes that exist because someone paid, dead gates as monuments, the game's wormholes as gates) are in the notes as proposed.

#### Where I'd Change r4

**1. "Links are found, not built" is gone everywhere.** Links are gates built against anchors, plus rare tears. "Owned by being known" became "owned by holding the anchor". r4's tells stay, and gates and tears both leak their far side. r4's hours split: a gate keeps its anchor's hours (the Ink's gates breathe with their pillar), and a tear keeps its own.

**2. The Ink is unreachable, not only unfindable.** r4 rested Adrasteia's first lock on secrecy. Secrecy is now the second reason; the first is that nobody else has an anchor in any known pillar. r4's arrival scene (you come out in fog and see a pillar far off) is reversed: you come out inside the pillar and are let down into the fog.

**3. Wonderland's "a rare link in a busy port's outer belt might open into hyperspace" is cut.** With gates, a link in a busy port belongs to somebody. A tear could still do it, and the core's rare tears are now "the best-kept secrets in Elysium" in [[Flatspace]].

#### Where I'd Change Canon

**1. [[Pseudospace]]'s corporations that "go all-in with the project of colonizing and exploiting Wonderland" are blind-gate bettors.** This is canon's own line read through your ruling; nothing in it changes.

**2. The [[FTL Trigger]] as humanity's first blind gate.** I put it in as a spacer saying, not a fact: the FTL drive was the first stab into the void, and it landed on Elysium. It fits canon (the drive tapped "aetheric forces" and shunted everyone) and your "tech long before comprehension". If it claims too much, it is one line to delete.

**3. Some tears may be other civilisations' abandoned gates.** Canon puts every civilisation that ever attempted FTL in Elysium. "Treasures we should have left alone" gets an owner this way. This is offered as a rumour only.

**4. The game's exit gate.** In code the run's exit is a wormhole that "leads nowhere". In this frame it reads as a blind gate or a tear. That is an observation for design, not a change.
