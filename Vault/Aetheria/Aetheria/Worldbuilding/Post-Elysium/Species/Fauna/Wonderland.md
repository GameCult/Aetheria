**"Everyone who goes in comes back with a story. Not everyone comes back."**

> Draft lore pass r4, 2026-10-14, revised r5 and r6, for the operator's review. Replaces r3's "Pseudospace Flavours". Part of [[Spaceborne Fauna]]. Wonderland is canon's name for pseudospace territory; this note gives it a shape: separate **layers**, each its own space with its own map, joined to each other and to [[Flatspace]] by **links**: gates someone paid for, and a few natural tears ([[Anchors and Gates]]). `[new]` marks invention that touches existing canon. Reasoning in [[Fauna Lore Review r4]], [[Fauna Lore Review r5]] and [[Fauna Lore Review r6]].

Canon defines [[Pseudospace]] as space whose geometry does not follow Euclid, and says that past the islands of flat space "absolutely anything is possible". Both are true. Wonderland is what a pilot meets in between: places where anything is possible but something in particular is true, reliably enough to learn, and strange enough that learning it changes you.

#### What a Layer Is

A layer is a whole space with its own rules: its own sky, its own physics, its own animals, and its own galaxy map. It is not a region of flatspace with odd weather. It is somewhere else, reached through a link, and the map screen shows it as its own layer [new].

What makes layers differ is the [[Aether]]'s temper, which canon says "varies with local conditions". Five questions tell layers apart (r3's dials, kept):

| Dial | The question | Flatspace's answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | How far does light carry, and what eats it? | Far; nothing eats it |
| **Heat** | Where does a ship's heat go? | Out, as light, which feeds the glim |
| **Surface** | Does the slope hold its shape? | Yes; charts stay true |
| **Flow** | What moves the medium? | A braided current |
| **Distance** | Does a kilometre stay a kilometre? | Yes |

Every layer gives a different answer to at least one of them, and each layer's animals live on its answers. Very little crosses from one layer to another, because what feeds an animal in one does not exist in the next.

#### Links

A link is a place where two spaces share an edge. Fly through and you are in the other one. Nearly every link is a **gate**: a ring someone built and paid for, opened onto an **anchor** someone carried to the far end the slow way. A few are **tears** nobody built. [[Anchors and Gates]] has the economics; what matters here is what a link means to a layer [new].

- **A layer is only as reachable as its anchors.** Humans opened gates long before they understood what the gates open into. A layer is known because somebody got in (through a tear, or a blind gate that came up lucky), crossed it, and planted an anchor. Every anchored gate into a layer is a record of that walk.
- **Links have tells.** A link leaks its far side. Near a link to the Ink the stars dim and radiators run too cool; near a link to the Glass the current slackens and dies; near one to the Fold the stars double. A pilot learns to taste the far side before committing. Gates and tears leak alike; only a tear leaks somewhere nobody has charted.
- **Some links keep hours.** A gate keeps its anchor's hours: the Ink's gates breathe with the pillars whose bowls their anchors sit in. A tear keeps its own: a beat, a season, a tide in the current. The fairy ring opens on the right night [new].
- **Links are owned by holding the anchor.** A faction holds a foothold in a layer by holding the anchors at the far ends of its links and keeping their tunings secret, and how well that works depends on the layer: an anchor in the Ink cannot be found, one in the Glass cannot be hidden, one in the Fold is in two places at once. Anchor registries are the most valuable documents in Elysium, and the best of them are never written down.

#### Depth

Depth in Wonderland is counted in links and in time, not kilometres. One gate from flatspace is shallow. Every link further, every step past the last anchor anyone keeps, and every day spent, takes you deeper. Going too deep is how people stop coming back: not because the way is barred, but because by then they no longer want the place they came from, or it no longer recognises them [new].

#### What Wonderland Does to People

Flatspace you can live in unchanged. Wonderland you cannot. Each layer works on the people inside it the way its rules work on light and heat: slowly, and in its own direction. The old fae legends are the right register: the folk under the hill keep their own law, a guest who eats their food may not leave, and those who return are gifted and marked, never just themselves again [new].

Each layer note gives what it takes and what it gives. In short:

| Layer | Takes | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| [[Adrasteia Space\|The Ink]] | Warmth. Returners are calm, quiet, uneasy in light | They feel heat the way others see light |
| [[The Glass]] | Privacy. Returners feel watched, forever, and cannot forget | They see how a disturbance travels; some become espers |
| [[The Fold]] | Certainty about who came back | They find the short way; they see the seam in things |
| [[Hyperspace]] | The way home | They know the roads; nobody else does |

#### Travellers' Tales

Layers become known the way Faerie did: by the stories of people who went and came back. A tale is evidence with no chart attached. It says what the place did to the teller, not where it is. Factions buy tales, insurers discount them, the [[Parallax Auditors]] subpoena them, and the people who tell them best are often the ones who went least deep [new].

In play, a tale is route knowledge in canon's sense: rumour, attributed, maybe manipulated, until somebody flies it.

#### The Layers So Far

| Layer | Its map | Foothold | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Adrasteia Space\|The Ink]] | Black; only the pillars shine | Adrasteia | A sea of fog that drinks light and heat; each zone is ruled by a pillar that does a sun's work in beats of dust |
| [[The Glass]] | Sharp and silver; old wakes drawn as lines | The Wavecrafters | Stiff Aether: no current, nothing fades, nowhere to hide |
| [[The Fold]] | Places drawn twice; edges glued | Nobody holds it | Knots where distance grows, seams that glue far places |
| [[Hyperspace]] | A disc, crowded at the rim, recentred on you | The beacon keepers | Hyperbolic room: every road is short and you will never find it |

More exist. Canon's "anything is possible" belongs to the layers nobody has a tale from yet, the ones only blind gates and tears have touched.

Spacers say: *taste the far side first*; *don't eat the food*; and, of anyone who came back too pleased, *ask her what it cost*.
