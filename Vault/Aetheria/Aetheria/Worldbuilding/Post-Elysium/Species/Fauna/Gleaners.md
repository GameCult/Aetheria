**"Loot fast. The gleaners are coming."**

> Draft r2, 2026-10-14. Part of [[Spaceborne Fauna]]; niche: scavenger, detritivore. New in r2. `[new]` marks invention that touches existing canon.

Fist-sized, armoured, all jaw and gut, moving in loose swarms of a few dozen. Gleaners eat what is dead: carcasses, wreckage, spilled cargo, the debris that spirals down every well. They are why wrecks in pseudospace do not last [new].

**Eats.** Organic matter first, then metal. Their gut grinds and concentrates metal from scrap and bone into dense pellets, the way some deep-sea animals concentrate minerals [new].

**Moves: the tell.** A swarm drifts with the current until it smells a fall, then swims straight to it. Gleaners moving against the current means something died upstream.

**What a pilot learns.** A swarm heading somewhere is a pointer to a fresh wreck, a whale fall, or a fight. Gleaners on a hull mean the hull is dead, or about to be.

**On sensors.** A cluster of tiny returns, like debris that knows where it is going.

**How it hurts.** They eat loot: dropped cargo and salvage drift in the current and gleaners strip it. A disabled or stationary ship gets nibbled: slow hull damage and lost external modules until it moves [new].

**Yield.** Gut-metal: refined metal pellets, worth more by weight than ore. Harvested by netting fat swarms after a feast.

**Lives.** Debris discs on the inner slopes of wells, where the drain carries everything; old battlefields; trailing behind whales; and anywhere something just died.

*Designers:* field drawn to wrecks and drops; competes with players for loot; hazard is attrition on stationary hulls.
