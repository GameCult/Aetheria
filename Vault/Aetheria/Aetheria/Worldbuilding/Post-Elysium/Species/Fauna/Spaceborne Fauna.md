**"The krill don't lie. Only the krill-shaped do."**

> Draft lore pass, 2026-10-13, for the operator's review. No fauna lore existed before this note, so everything here is new. `[new]` marks an invented fact that touches existing canon; a wikilink marks the canon it leans on. Class notes: [[Krill]], [[Minnows]], [[Sharks]], [[Whales]].

Elysium's void is not empty. Between the wells of every system humanity reaches drift clouds of krill, schools of minnows, sharks that cut against the current, and whales that swing in arcs wide enough to take a day. Spacers named them after Earth animals because that was the vocabulary they had. None of them is related to anything from Sol, and nothing like them ever lived there.

#### What They Are

The fauna are native life of [[Pseudospace]]. Humanity did not bring them; Sol never had anything this strange. They were waiting on the far side of the first wormholes opened after the Rupture, and they have been on the far side of every wormhole since.

They are vacuum life shaped by the [[Aether]]. Their tissues are thin, cold and full of empty cells. They feed on starlight and on heat gradients through a slow, biological cousin of [[Negative Entropy]] [new], turning warmth into light and light into growth. Traces of the strange matter that negentropy needs run through their bodies, the larger the animal the more [new]. They have no lungs and no water. Most of them are mostly empty space held together by membrane.

They are honest things. A krill cloud is what it looks like, moves the way krill move, and burns as bright as krill burn. Nothing in the ecology mimics anything else. That is why a ship can hide among them, and why it can be found: every class has one way of moving, and a hull that is pretending has to fly that way by hand.

#### Where They Came From

The Rupture threw Sol into Elysium as a single zone: a cloudless bubble of flat space, with the [[Aether]] pressing in at its Oort cloud. Sol is the island of sanity. For a while humanity lived only inside it. The fauna arrived with the wormholes [new].

When the factions began opening wormholes to reach new systems, every mouth they opened let a little of the outside in. [[Pseudospace]] is the natural state of Elysium; flat space is the island humanity stands on. The wormholes are where the island touches the sea, and the fauna are what lives in the sea. They come through the mouths in slow tides and spread out along the gravity slopes of every system the network reaches [new].

What this means in practice:

- **No fauna inside Sol, ever.** Sol's bubble is cloudless and barren. The strangeness begins at its Oort edge, where the Aether presses in: the first fauna a Sol-born pilot ever sees are the thin krill hazes that cling to the outside of the bubble, glinting against nothing [new]. Past that, fauna thicken with every jump outward.
- **Toward the edge of stable space they get bigger and stranger.** Core systems have krill and minnows. Frontier systems, where the Chaos factions live, have sharks and whales. Past the edge of what anyone has charted, spacers say there are larger things, and they are probably right [new].
- **They never run out.** A harvested ground refills because the wormholes keep feeding it. The tide from the far side is slow but it does not stop [new].
- **Nobody controls them.** No faction made them, owns their genome or can switch them off. In a setting where almost every living thing has a manufacturer, the fauna are the one resource that arrived without an owner, and everyone is racing to become one.

#### The Shape of the Ecology

Everything follows gravity. The fauna feed where light is strong and the gravity slope is gentle enough to hold a cloud together without tearing it apart. Their placement draws the map of the wells.

- **Krill** are the floor of the chain. They ride the orbital flow and bloom on the shoulder of each well: not deep in, where tidal shear tears clouds apart, and not far out, where light is thin and nothing holds them. Where two wells pull equally, in the saddles and trojan points between bodies, krill pool and stretch into filaments that link one body's cloud to the next. Seen at system scale, the krill draw a web between the wells, thickest along the gravity ridgelines and thickest of all near the wormhole mouths. Pilots call it the net.
- **Minnows** graze krill. They school along the downstream edges of the clouds and follow the filaments from well to well.
- **Sharks** hunt minnows. They hang on the steep side of a well and fall in to strike, using the planet's pull for their sprint.
- **Whales** filter krill in bulk. They live around gas giants, where the slope is broad and the clouds are biggest, and swing between the giant and its moons in long, slow arcs that cut through the net.
- **Suns** feed everything and host nothing large. Close in, light is rich but the slope is a cliff. Sunward clouds are thin, fast and bright.
- **Wormhole mouths** are springs. Fresh krill pour out of them; the net is densest within a few filaments of a mouth and thins with distance from it.

A pilot reads a system the way a fisher reads a coast:

| What you see | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Thick krill, steady sway | Nothing big nearby. Calm water. |
| A clean channel through a cloud | A whale passed, or a trawler did. A trawler leaves straight edges. |
| A lush slope with no fauna at all | It was harvested recently and will refill. Or something hot is sitting there. |
| Minnows balled up tight and dim | A shark is close. |
| Sharks circling a krill cloud | Something warm is inside it. |
| A school parting around nothing | A hull is hiding in the gap. |
| Fauna streaming in toward one point | A whale fall. |
| Bigger animals than the system should have | You are nearer the edge than the chart says. |

#### Elysium's Harvest

Sol spent a thousand years fighting over dwindling rocks. Elysium's wormholes opened onto a renewable sea, and the survivors organized it the only way they knew: claims, permits, contracts and debt [new].

- **What is taken.** Krill paste feeds the regeneration of [[Bloom]] habitats' living concrete, which every habitat needs forever. Minnow photophores become flares and sensor glass. Shark heat-organs are the best radiators money can buy. Whale bone carries the strange matter that [[Negative Entropy]] needs, until now found only in Adrasteia-controlled pseudospace, and Adrasteia buys every gram so that nobody else learns to use it [new].
- **Who takes it.** [[VitaForge]] buys most of the krill and owns the processing [new]. [[Orbital Forge]] retooled its Resonant Shatter Swarm resonators into fishing gear: tuned pulses that stun a school without breaking it. Resonance fishing is the dominant method and an Orbital Forge licensing business [new]. [[Lightsail Express]] hauls the catch. [[Zhestokost]] arms and armours its hulls in shark hide. Independent pilots do the dangerous middle work: raking krill, netting minnows, hunting sharks for bounty. The richest grounds are out toward the frontier, which pulls harvesters outward and puts them in the Chaos factions' space.
- **How it is governed.** The [[Pan-Solar Consortium]] issues harvest claims on the model of the [[Conflict Permit]]: a ground, a season, a tonnage [new]. Everyone breaks them. Leading sharks into a rival's krill grounds is a recognized form of [[Resource Denial]] [new].
- **Why it never runs out.** Grounds refill from the wormholes. The tide is slow, so a ground stripped today is barren for a while, and claim wars are fought over timing, not stock. Whoever holds a wormhole mouth holds the spring.

#### Spacers and the Fauna

- **Names.** *The net* (the krill web). *Calm water* (thick, undisturbed krill). *Springs* (wormhole mouths, as feeding grounds). *Bait ball* (minnows balled by a shark). *A fall* (a dead whale and everything feeding on it). *Raking*, *netting*, *flensing* (harvesting krill, minnows, whales).
- **Going krill.** Hiding a ship in a cloud by matching the flow. It is cheap and everyone knows it, so *dead krill* (a cloud member that doesn't sway) and *hot krill* (one that glows like a reactor) are the first things a hunter looks for.
- **Wearing the school.** Hiding among minnows. It is harder, because minnows mill fast and a ship can't.
- **The hunter's patience.** Count the sway. Watch a return through one full cycle of its class's motion before you trust it. Shooting to check announces you.
- **Superstitions.** Never kill the first whale you see on a run. A shark that won't hunt is a ship. If the net is empty in calm water, turn around. A school that parts for you is luck; a school that parts for nothing is a warning. Don't fish within sight of a mouth after a jump; something bigger may have come through with you.
- **The fauna as witnesses.** Fauna never lie, and sharks hunt heat. A disguised ship running hot inside a krill cloud draws sharks, and the sharks give it away. Hunters sometimes wait for the sharks.

#### The Navigators and the Whales

Humanity's whales and Elysium's whales share a name and nothing else. [[GeneSys]] uplifted Earth's cetaceans to read the currents of space, and the FTL drive that caused the Rupture routed itself through models of cetacean cognition. The [[Cetacean Navigators]] were the first to read the currents through the new wormholes, and the first crews to meet the animals living in them [new].

They do not call them whales. Their name translates roughly as *the slow singers* [new]. Navigators read the singers' migrations as charts of the currents beyond the mouths and mark their repeated arcs as whale roads [new]. Their position is that the question of whether the slow singers are persons is open, and in a setting where personhood is disputed and owned, that is a political position. Navigator space allows no whaling, only salvage from natural falls [new], which puts them in quiet conflict with Adrasteia's buyers.

Whale song runs through the [[Aether]]. [[Esper]]s hear it, and so does the [[Aethernet]], where a singing whale is noise in the one signal space everyone shares. The Queen's Guard would like the whales quieter [new].

#### Room for More

The classes are four to start, and the ecology has gaps meant for more: *void spiders* that string nets across trojan points to catch drifting krill [new; the operator's 2020 "space whales and space spiders" line]; gas giant drifters that never leave the upper cloud deck; and whatever lives past the edge of the charts, where pseudospace stops pretending to be space.
