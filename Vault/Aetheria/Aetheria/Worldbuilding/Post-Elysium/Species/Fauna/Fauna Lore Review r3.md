#### What r3 Did

- Made pseudospace flavours a framework ([[Wonderland]]): five dials (light, heat, surface, flow, distance), set by the stars, each flavour with its own bestiary and its own crossing tells. Four flavours: **Open Water** (r2's ecology, now a family from Shallows to Deeps), **the Ink** (Adrasteia space), **the Glass** (stiff Aether: no current, nothing fades, nowhere to hide) and **the Fold** (non-Euclidean: knots where distance grows, seams that glue far places).

#### Where I'd Change Your Sketch

**1. The fog should drink heat, not only light.** r2 already has the Aether turning a little heat into light everywhere. If the Ink's fog eats light, it eats that light too, so heat drains away: radiators work too well and everything is cold. That one consequence does three jobs. It gives the Ink a tell on the thermal display before the stars go out. It makes heat the Ink's scarcest food, which is why its animals hunt ships. And it makes the Ink the place Adrasteia's fear of the cold death comes from. Without it, the fog is a visual effect with no ecology behind it.

**2. The danger should scale with the dust.** Your sketch says the beasts accumulate dust and are dangerous. I'd make one the cause of the other. Dust does negent: inside a body it turns heat into usable light, so a dusted animal can eat heat, and more dust means it eats more heat from further away. Then biomagnification does the rest: a trace in the waves, more in each step up, most in the apex. The richest dust is always inside the deadliest animal. I'd also add one way to get rich dust without a kill, [[Cachalots|grey]], on the model of ambergris, so the harvest has a patient trade and not only a lethal one.

**3. The pillar should be where the stars' light goes.** "Stars that connect to a pillar" read as a picture. I'd give it a cause: in a fog that eats light a star cannot shine outward, so its light leaves up the pillar, which is the visible star. That makes the pillars the Ink's only landmarks and its only navigation, and it makes a pillar's pulse its signature. I chose stars strung on the pillar like beads, the lowest at the surface; your words also allow one star per pillar. Either works.

**4. "Adrasteia space" should be a flavour, not one place.** Your ruling calls it a flavour, and a flavour is a kind of place, so there can be more than one Ink. Canon says the strange matter is found only in Adrasteia-controlled pseudospace. I'd keep that sentence true by saying every Ink anyone has found, Adrasteia was already in. That adds a mystery (how do they find them first?) rather than spending one. Whether there is one Ink or many is a real fork: it changes the map and the story's endgame. It is question `aetheria-fauna:question:ink-one-or-many` in the mind.

**5. A tension with the engine ruling, to settle before anyone writes the swell into play.** The lore says ships ride the Ink's swell. Ruling `liquid-grid-from-sim-brushes` makes the fine grid presentation configured from the sim, and Self's reading is that gravity stays analytic and a shallow-water layer decides nothing. If the swell never moves a ship, the lore should say the swell carries dust and animals but a hull is too heavy to feel it. That is a one-line change to [[Adrasteia Space]], and I'd wait for the engine to decide rather than write either version as fact.

**Self's reading, for the record.** Its two calls hold and are in the notes: the dust is stellar and the beasts concentrate it; the pressure pockets are the only clear water. I extended both. Bioaccumulation alone makes animals that hold dust, not animals that are dangerous because of it, so the dust needed a use (item 2). Pockets are clear water and also the only warm water, which is why everything rides them and why predators hunt their walls.

#### Where I'd Change r2

**1. One sea was the wrong frame.** r2 designed a single ecology with a depth gradient. That ecology was right and is now **Open Water**, a family of flavours: the Shallows near flat space to the Deeps toward the Chaos factions. "Deeper is richer" still holds within the family. The Ink, the Glass and the Fold are not deeper water; they are other water.

**2. Wild negent is now load-bearing.** r2 proposed that the Aether does negent weakly everywhere, as the energy budget of Open Water. In r3 the same sentence sets the heat dial of every flavour: the light escapes in Open Water, is eaten in the Ink, never forms in the Glass. It has gone from a nice idea to the hinge of the framework, so it now needs its canon edit (below) rather than living only in fauna notes.

**3. The whale-bone proposal.** Cut, as ruled. What it was trying to buy, a harvest that matters to the monopoly and a conflict over who takes it, the Ink now buys from its own animals: skimmers, hunters and grey-pickers working on vouches, selling to Adrasteia's blind drops.

#### Where I'd Change Canon

**1. [[Pseudospace]]: "absolutely anything is possible" needs a grammar, and its definition needs honouring.** Canon defines pseudospace as space whose geometry is not Euclidean, then says anything is possible. Both are true and neither is playable: a pilot cannot learn "anything". I'd add that the known reaches come in flavours with reliable rules, and keep "anything is possible" for what lies past them. And canon's own definition is honoured by almost nothing in the notes, or as far as I know the game, until now. The Fold is the flavour where distance itself bends, and I'd treat it as the flavour that makes the Pseudospace note true in play. Your remark that you once wanted non-Euclidean space and weren't sure how to bend the engine is why it is in this set; the note describes how it flies, not how to build it.

**2. [[Negative Entropy]]: two sentences.** Add that the Aether does the conversion weakly everywhere unaided, and that strange matter does it strongly (r2's wild negent, now the heat dial). And reword "encountered only in Adrasteia-controlled pseudospace" to "found only in the Ink, and every Ink anyone has found is Adrasteia's". Same meaning, and it points at the mystery.

**3. Faction Play's tell inverts in the Ink.** "A contact colder than its surroundings is dead or Adrasteian" is right in Open Water. In the Ink everything is colder than you, and the warm contact is the stranger. One line in the Adrasteia section.

**4. [[Aether]]: "varies with local conditions" is where flavours live.** Canon's static reference frame survives: it tells you how far you have moved, which is why you can always know where you are inside the Ink and never where the Ink is (its mouths are kept and their far ends are uncharted). In the Fold, the frame is how pilots measure depth into a knot. I'd add one line naming flavours as the local conditions.

**5. Smaller.** Canon calls Adrasteia "the Adrasteia corporation"; the brainstorm's society of vouches and no addresses doesn't sound like a corporation. Not this pass's business, but the Ink makes the question louder.
