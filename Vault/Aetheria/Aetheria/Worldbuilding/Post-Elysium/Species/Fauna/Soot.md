**"Soot on the swell means the pocket just passed. You're late."**

> Draft r3, 2026-10-14. Part of [[Adrasteia Space]] (the Ink); niche: producer. `[new]` marks invention that touches existing canon.

Black lace on the moving surface of the Ink: a colonial film, finer than net, that spreads across the swell in the wake of each dust wave and sinks again before the next. In a pocket's light it glints where dust grains are caught in it.

**Eats.** The heat of the dust. A wave's dust leaves the pillar warm and cools as it travels; soot spreads behind each wave and drinks that warmth through the trace of strange matter it catches, the way [[Glim]] eats light in Open Water. It is the only thing in the Ink that lives on the waves directly. Everything else lives on soot or on what eats it [new].

**Moves: the tell.** It doesn't. It blooms in a ring behind each pocket, thickest where the pocket closed, and fades as the ring cools. A soot ring is a clock: how fresh it is says how long since the last wave passed.

**What a pilot learns.** Soot is how you read the beat in fog. Thick, fresh soot means a pocket just went by and the next is a full beat away. Thin, old soot means the next wave is close.

**On sensors.** Nothing at range. Close in, a faint cold sheen on the surface, colder than the fog.

**How it hurts.** Barely. It fouls intakes and nets, and a ship that sits in thick soot loses heat a little faster. It is dangerous mainly for what it draws: [[Rays]] come to graze it, and [[Squid]] follow the rays.

**Yield.** *Soot-ash*: skimmed, dried and sold through blind drops. A trace of dust in a great deal of ash. It is the Ink's safe harvest, and the one most vouched independents ever take.

**Lives.** Everywhere a dust wave has been, in rings around every pillar.

*Designers:* a ring field that blooms behind each pulse and fades by the next; a heat-sink patch; a readable indicator of the pulse phase.
