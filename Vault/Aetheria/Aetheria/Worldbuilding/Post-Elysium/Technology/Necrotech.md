##### Necrotech

Some corporations leverage the potential of [[Aether]]ic spirit creation ([[Spirits]]) in the ethically dubious practice of breeding genetically engineered human espers and harvesting them en masse to imprint their souls into technological products. These can accomplish roughly the same effects as specialized embodied [[Artificial General Intelligence]], but require regular refueling if they aren’t designed to be disposable. They are more generalizable and faster to develop since they do not require the extensive work of iterating on embodied AGI minds, but greater per-unit costs means they lose out in economies of scale for mass-produced items.

The aether answers only to a mind that wants something, and a will cannot be simulated, so it has to be harvested. That is the whole economic case for the necrofactory.

### The Spectrum

Not every imprint is a person. An imprinted Aetheric Resonance Interface is not quite conscious, and the ones the factories churn out are much less so: a single recorded intention, playing on a loop, used to automate cheap, reproducible aetheric effects. You might have one in your fridge to improve its Carnot efficiency, a manufactured ghost in the machine sitting right next to the brainwashed upload that turns it into a smart fridge, the one that orders groceries when you run low and compliments your children on their terrible artwork when you stick it to the door with a magnet.

ARIs exist on a spectrum of complexity, and price climbs with how far an effect bends reality:

- **Factory stamps** do things too small for anyone to notice or contest: the fridge, a quieter heat exchanger, a battery that sags a little less.
- **Imprinted ARIs** keep more of their source mind and can push harder, as long as they do not contradict what the people around them expect. They cost more to make and more to keep fed.
- **Bound powerful espers** are the only ARIs that can hold an effect against consensus, and only they retain some semblance of comprehensible mortal habits. The habits are not a side effect. They are the part of the mind that can still push.

### Perception

As with other questionable and horrifying corporate practices, a lot of effort is spent in managing public perception of the technology. Only countercultural dissidents ever call it necrotech or their production lines necrofactories. The corporations themselves prefer to label them *Aetheric Resonance Interfaces*, making it sound like an advanced energy phenomenon rather than forced spirit entrapment. The media is flooded with propaganda explaining how necrotech is "completely humane" and "scientifically proven" to be non-conscious. For nearly every unit sold that is technically true, which is what makes it such an effective lie: it says nothing about the top of the spectrum, or about the espers bred and spent to make the cheap ones.
